The game can now speak rival barks (the maintainer, 2026-10-01: "Voices go in"). When a bark's subtitle shows, the bubble sends a `throttlebrawl:bark` event and audio plays that line's clip on the voices bus, found by the line's content reference (`<pack>:bark-set/<set>#<line>` is spoken by `packs/<pack>/assets/audio/barks/<set>/<line>.ogg`). The music dips a little under a voice (`audio.voiceDuck`, 0.7, lighter than a crash's 0.4), one voice speaks at a time, and the subtitle stays up until its voice finishes; a bark that comes while another rival is still mid-line waits for that voice (up to 2.5 s, else it is dropped), so no line is cut off. The narrative-bubble browser test now accepts a bubble that a voice holds up or the next line replaces. A line cut with "cut this" never speaks again and stops at once if it is speaking (every veto already reaches audio through `setRadioCut`). `setVoices({ on, volume })` takes the Voices switch and level from the settings record once it carries `voicesOn` and `voiceVolume`; until then the defaults (on, 0.8) apply, and `audio.voiceGain` is a tuning slider on top. Clips load the first time their line is said, from a lazy chunk, never in the first load; a missing, slow or broken clip leaves the subtitle silent. Audio is presentation only, so the sim and replays are untouched. The clips themselves land in the next change.
