The Medium and Low graphics settings now really lighten the picture when a phone struggles. Before, Low only pulled the distant scenery in a little, which saved about 3% at the busiest spot in the game. Now Low draws the far houses and trees as simple shapes much sooner, plants fewer trees and ferns, draws the small roadside things (bins, meters, parked cars, lane lines) in full detail only close by, and simplifies the far city blocks. At each region's busiest view that is about a quarter lighter. The buildings, landmarks, signs, the road and the skyline stay, so every place still reads as itself, and nothing that moves or can hit you is ever trimmed. High looks exactly as before. Not tested on the benchmark phone yet.

For devs (playtest 4 run C's live check, punch item 9; roadmap M5).

- **The cut per tier** (`QUALITY_TIERS` in `render/quality.ts`; default sliders): `medium` reach 324 m, the scatter's far stand-ins from 100 m, 70% of the trees and fern clumps, the roadside's understory to 35 m, its street fronts in full to 56 m and its middling props (parked cars among them) to 84 m, lane lines and posts to 210 m, a downtown's stand-ins from 300 m and Chinatown's from 144 m. `low`: 288 m; 30 m; 40%; 20, 32 and 48 m; 120 m; 150 and 72 m. `high` keeps 1 on every new field, so it draws what it drew.
- **New seams.** `scenery-merge.ts`: the thinnable kinds (`THINNABLE_KINDS`: palms, mangroves, conifers, coast trees, brush; never a building, battery or outcrop) lead each block's buffer by `thinRank` (a hash of where each stands), near pass in order and far pass reversed, so a tree share is still one draw range per block, and each tier's trees are a subset of the next one's. `MergedScenery.update` and `RoadScene.update` take the share; `RoadScene.update` also takes the share of `ROAD_FINE_DRAW_M`. `roadside.ts` `levelOf` scales its three distances. `verge.ts` `setTreeShare` thins the fern clumps by the same rank. `downtown.ts` now builds each stretch as [models | surfaces | the models' far stand-ins] (`formsOf`) and draws the stand-ins past `nearM` (default: never); `chinatown-northbeach.ts` `update` takes its `NEAR_M` as a parameter. `render/index.ts` passes the tier's values each frame.
- **Not tiered, on purpose.** Traffic, pedestrians and riders are sim entities: their counts are part of the seeded race and they are threats ("tiers never hide threats"), so no tier changes them. The land, the road surfaces and the backdrop (a place's skyline, bridges and far landmarks) are untouched. Blob shadows are one instanced draw of at most 64 14-triangle ovals (896 triangles), not worth a tier.
- **Measured** (the still scene's peak view, `scene-cost`'s sweep: every 5 m of every road a route allows, both ways, six cameras, seed 1). New test `src/render/quality-cost.test.ts` holds `low` at least 25% under `high` on each region's heaviest route: Russian Hill 105,269 to 76,365 triangles (27.5%), the Gorge 93,639 to 69,510 (25.8%), the Keys' M1 standard run 89,418 to 65,812 (26.4%). Its control rides Russian Hill with the low tier as it was (reach only): 96,153, 8.7% lighter, under the bar. Other routes, from a one-off sweep of all 23 at all three tiers: I-5 22.3%, the Pacific Northwest's invented roads 19.7%, Bridge City 16.7%, the invented San Francisco hills 13.3%, Twin Peaks 11.2%, Chinatown and North Beach 11.0%, Duval 5.8%, downtown 4.5%, Golden Gate 3.8%, the Seven Mile 2.5%, the Mission 1.5%. Where the land and the backdrop are most of the frame, there is less to cut without them.
- **High is unchanged.** `scene-cost*.test.ts` on main and on this branch print the same peak triangles and draw calls on all 23 routes. No route draws more calls on a lower tier (all 23, peak-draw view).
- **Headroom.** Draw calls: the held views' worst is 77 of the still scene's 80, and the helmet view's (printed, not held) 82, both unchanged. Triangles: the still scene's worst at `high` is Russian Hill's 105,269 of 110,000 (4,731 under, unchanged); at `low` the worst of all 23 routes is Twin Peaks' 77,033 (32,967 under). With about 33,000 for what moves (#609's estimate), Russian Hill's whole frame would go from about 138,000 to about 109,000 on `low`: an estimate, not a browser measurement. First-load JavaScript: 497,264 bytes gzip (485.6 KB of 500 KB, 14.4 KB headroom), +471 bytes against main's 496,793 (both built on the dev machine).
- Tests: `quality-cost.test.ts` (new); `scenery-merge.test.ts` (a block of 10 houses and 40 trees draws exactly the expected props, near and far, at shares 1, 0.7, 0.4 and 0; it failed with the far pass not reversed), `quality.test.ts` (each lower tier cuts every new field; `high`'s reach is the sliders' with every share 1), `roadside.test.ts`, `downtown.test.ts`, `chinatown-northbeach.test.ts` and `verge.test.ts` (a lower tier draws less at one spot, and the top tier exactly what the default drew). Also run: `scene-cost`, `scene-cost-sf`, `scene-cost-pnw`, `render`, `portland-blocks`; typecheck; lint. `tests/timings.json` prices the new file at 42 s (its local time); the unit plan is 263 s per slice against the 336 s line.

Docs: architecture.md "Quality tiers and dynamic resolution" (the cuts, what stays, the measured cut) and engineering.md's perf check (the new test).

Not checked: the phone (done, not phone-verified), no browser was run, and how the thinner trees and nearer stand-ins look at speed; a playtest's call.
