The Keys look more like the Keys. A cruise ship is tied up behind Mallory Square's pier at the end of Duval Street: a white wall of balconies beside the finish. Ospreys nest on tall posts along the shore and mangrove stretches of the highway, a few hundred metres apart. And the sea is no longer one flat colour: it is shallow turquoise over the flats, with darker patches of seagrass and paler patches of sand, and a deep blue in the channel under the Seven Mile Bridge's hump and under the other raised spans.

For devs: the ship is the `cruise-ship` landmark of `osm-duval-street` (`keys-landmarks#cruise_ship`, Codex CX6), a 290 by 36 m box from s 1489 to 1779 and d -76 to -40 on the Gulf side, with `baseY` 0 so its root stands at the waterline (the street is a metre over the sea) and `farM` 300. It is in the road file and in the bake config's road features, placed by hand in baked metres: `src/render/cruise-ship.test.ts` checks it is beyond the pier's tip and abreast of it, over open water past the land the road scene draws (`landReach`) and 20 m clear of every road, inside its box with its hull 8 m under and its top 60 m over the sea, and on its 64-triangle far hull past `farM`, each with a control. The osprey post (`keys_osprey_post`, variant 8 of `keysIdentity`) is the `osprey-post` rule at the end of `KEYS_KIT` (shore and mangrove sides, every 420 m at half the chances, never in Old Town, so no other rule's props move). `keysIdentity` now loads for any Keys network with a `water-open` tag, and takes the shared decode with its landmark view (`LANDMARK_ROOTS`, the mile post), so a Seven Mile race, which loads both, gets one result either way (`src/render/osprey-post.test.ts`: both load orders, the kit asked for only where there is open water, posts on drawn land past the verge and a territory apart, none in Old Town or without the rule). `scenery.test.ts` now expects 9 variants of that kit; `duval.test.ts` finds a landmark node as the layer does (`_lod0`).

The sea (`src/render/sea-bands.ts`, new): a tropical network with water beside a road draws its sea as one vertex-coloured mesh named `road-water` in place of the plane (968 triangles, one draw call, whatever the route's length): 56 m squares about the camera, snapped and re-laid when it crosses one, inside a ring out to the old plane's extent. The tints multiply the look's water colour through a new material param, `paletteBase`, so a region palette still recolours the sea. Deep water is read from the bake (any deck over 5 m, full at 11 m: the Seven Mile's 17 m hump makes a channel about 500 m across, Bahia Honda's and Spanish Harbor's spans theirs), the open sea gets a little deeper far from land, and the flats carry seeded seagrass and sand patches. No texture, nothing per route. Duval (no water tag) and every non-tropical network keep the one plane. `src/render/sea-bands.test.ts` has the rules and controls.
