Six landmarks that did not read now do (playtest 4). Coit Tower is a real tower on the way up Lombard Street: it stands dead ahead at the end of the long flat stretch, 80 m tall, above the row houses, instead of a grey shaft cut off at the edge of the screen at the finish. Bridge City's roof sign shows its whole board from the bridge down West Burnside (a row of fronts no longer stands in front of it), and its salmon and frame now glow like neon instead of drawing as a thin dark line. The Golden Gate toll gantry's board says something, "TOLL BY PLATE. SMILE." (it was showing its blank back), and the Golden Gate has suspender ropes hanging from its cables. Pioneer Courthouse Square sits under a grey rain cloud with rain falling from it, so it reads as a square from the road. The Seven Mile's repair platforms stand on pipe piles you can see from the deck, not on nothing.

The toll gantry's line is invented, in the pack as a vetoable sign. One taste call for the maintainer: Coit Tower moved from the circle at the finish to the foot of the hill, and moving it back is one feature.
