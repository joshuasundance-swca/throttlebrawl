The Keys are easier to read at speed, and Duval no longer shows the sea through its buildings. The Seven Mile Bridge's mile posts are bigger green boards with a big number stacked down them (the word "MILE" is small along the top), so you can read the mile as you pass. Big Pine's Key deer now stand in clearings you can see into, not behind sea grape, in small groups that step back from the road one behind another, and their coats are darker so they stand out from the sand. Smathers Beach's inland salt ponds are now a murky olive-green with a muddy edge, so the sea on one side of the road and the ponds on the other look like two different waters (a default; the maintainer can veto the colour). Duval Street's second row of houses is now whole, so a slanting view down the street no longer finds a gap with the sea in it.

For devs: four punch items from the playtest-4 fix check of run B (lane G2), each tested with a control. (1) Mile posts: the number was one small line ("MILE 4x"), about 4 device px tall at 16 m on a DPR-2 phone. `text-surfaces.ts` gets a `stack` style: the words' last token is the number, set as a column of big digits under a small word, so a tall narrow board gives the number its whole height, and `keys_mile_marker_face` uses it; the seven baked posts say `scale` 3 (was 2; the bake config and `tools/gis` agree: the schema allows up to 4). A digit is 16.9 device px tall at 16 m on a DPR-2 phone, 915 by 412 (floor 14; the one-line board is 3.6 px, the control; `tests/sim/keys-readability.test.ts`). A tall board's cell on the shared canvas is now 512 px tall and as narrow as the board (it was the canvas's full width, 1,792 px tall, so seven posts stood a 12,600 px canvas; now about 3,650, under what a small phone takes as a texture), and the layer maps a board's u across its own cell. (2) Key deer: new `sight` and `lean` rule options. Both deer rules run first (`first`, so no other rule's random stream moves) and keep a 14 m lane to the road clear (the sea grape and trees, placed after, grow round it); a herd (the `key-deer-herd` rule, across now 4 to 4.8 m) steps 1.1 m toward the road a section at a time, down to the ridable band, so a row of deer along the verge does not hide each one behind the next. From the chase camera at 24, 16 and 10 m before each deer, 5 of 5 body points show past any bush or tree on seeds 1, 7, 42 and 99 (the old placement: 24 to 36 half-hidden views a seed, the control) and at least 3 of 5 past the herd's own deer. Herds: 2 to 3 a seed, of 3 to 6 deer; 13 to 22 deer a seed (was 18 to 22). Their coat is deepened at the bake (`render/key-deer.ts`, called by `bakeModel` for the `keysIdentity` variants 0 and 1; the file is untouched): 94 to 95 % of it is at least 3:1 against the sand (`CLASSIC_PALETTE.land`), where the file's own coat is 12 to 15 %. The verge-gap test now allows up to 2.5 m past the ridable ground (a herd's first deer), and "moves nothing else" is now "moves little else": the props that differ from the scene without deer (12 to 26 of about 950 to 980 a seed) are all within the lane's reach of a deer. (3) Smathers' salt ponds: a new side tag `salt-pond` (the three runs of `water-shallow` on the inland side say it too; `tools/gis` lint lists it; docs/content-packs.md). `sea-bands.ts` paints a span's inland water out to 90 m from the road's edge pale olive (`#a8b47c`) with a mud edge (`#6f5f43`) by the road, at the ends and on the far side, from the same tint function: no new mesh, no new draw call. [default] colours: the pond sits 40 on the Lab scale from the nearest of 855 sea colours (shallows, sand and seagrass patches, open water, the Seven Mile's deep channel; floor 25), and the control (the tag taken off) is a sea colour. (4) Duval's second row (`oldtown-back`): the eye is 3 m up, so a ray slants through a gap in the front row and comes out past the second row where the front was closed (no house stood behind a closed front), and a lot or a slit in the second row opened each of those. The row is whole now: `behindOpenM: 0` (new rule option; the default is half a house open, as before), a gap of -0.6 to 0 m, no empty lot. `tests/sim/duval-sight.test.ts` casts 84 rays through the outer 40 % of each side of the real chase camera's frame at 38 m/s on the phone's screen, 230 views along both streets and the window round s 288: the views that reach the sea went from 22 to 28 a seed to none (seeds 1, 7 and 42), and with no buildings 12 of 84 rays do (the control).

Budgets: no new draw call anywhere (the ponds are the sea mesh's tint, the second row and the deer ride the roadside stretches, the board is the surface layer's one quad). Triangles: Duval's busiest view is now 87,922 (was 73,972; +13,950 for the whole second row) with 57 draw calls (unchanged), against the sweep's 110,000 and 120 draw calls; the Seven Mile sweep's peak is 67,914 triangles and Key West's 73,882 (both measured with this change). First-load JavaScript (gzip level 9, `vite build`, scripts/first-load.mjs's rule): 499,129 bytes (487.4 KB) against 498,690 (487.0 KB) on the base commit built the same way, so +439 bytes (all in the entry chunk) and 12.6 KB of the 500 KB budget left (the 10 KB floor for a PR that grows it holds). Not done: the pond's colour on the phone's ink look, which was not seen (no browser was run: done, not phone-verified), and a rendered check of the herds' look from the chase camera (the occlusion test is geometric, on the triangles).
