The Seven Mile Bridge now looks like the Seven Mile Bridge. Pigeon Key, the five-acre island under the old bridge where the railroad's work crews once lived, sits in the water beside the old road with its two yellow clapboard cottages, its dock and a few palms, and boats keep clear of it. And the Overseas Highway has its green mile posts: one every mile, each with its own number, on your right just outside the rail. The bridge starts at mile 46.804, so you pass 46, 45 and on down to 41 on the span and 40 as you land on Little Duck Key. The posts face the way you are coming, so the number is the first thing they show you. Our thanks to Wikipedia's articles "Pigeon Key" (where the island is: 24.703991 N, 81.155308 W) and "Overseas Highway" (the bridge runs from mile 46.804 to mile 40.011).

For devs: Pigeon Key is the landmark `seven-mile-kit#pigeon_key`, composed in `landmarks.ts` (`COMPOSITES`) from `src/render/pigeon-key.ts` (new: the island's sand and grass plate, sunk at the rim, with six palms, about 830 triangles) and the Seven Mile kit's `pigeon_key_cottage_a`, `pigeon_key_cottage_b` and `pigeon_key_dock` (Codex CX2, already in the file). The island is drawn beside the old road, not under its deck: the bake centres its 110 by 54 m box on the article's point, 48.5 m off the old road's line on the new highway's side, so the lint's `landmark-clear` passes without `overRoad` and nothing of it meets a pier or a repair platform. The cottages face the road and the dock runs out to open water. The landmark takes two new params, read by `landmarkParams`: `island` (boats, skiffs and islets keep 10 m off its box: `scenery.ts` `islandBoxes` and `onIsland`, used by `road-mesh.ts` `openWater`) and `number` (a per-instance number). Both are in the road lint (`validate.ts`) and in the bake's `Landmark` model (`network.py`).

The kit's file is the bays' file too, and the manifest loads each id once and hands its first decode to every caller, so a second decode of `models/scenery/seven-mile-kit` would have got the first one's value. `bakeModel` now also bakes the file's landmark roots (`LANDMARK_ROOTS`, `SceneryModel.landmarkNodes`) and `loadLandmarkKits` reads them from the scenery decode, in either order (`pigeon-key.test.ts` loads them both ways). `keys-identity` (the CX5 Keys kit) is a landmark kit with its own decode for now; whoever registers it as a scenery kind must give it the same shared decode.

Mile posts: the bake's new line-level `mileposts` (`network.py` `Milepost`, `milepost_features`): the mile number at a real point, the spacing (1,609.344 m), the side, the offset from the centre line (5.85 m on a bridge, 10 m on land, where the verge is wider) and the facing (`yawDeg` 180, so the board looks back at the rider). It writes one `landmark` feature per post, `mile-46` and so on, with `params.number`; on the Seven Mile that is 7 posts on 3 roads. A post that would stand in a junction's connector is left out. The words are the Keys region's sign `keys-mile-marker-face` ("MILE {n}", `site`, `surface`, `new`), through a new seam in `text-surfaces.ts`: a surface with a `number` fills the sign's `{n}`, each distinct number is its own row of the shared canvas, and one cut of the sign blanks every post. Still one draw call for all the surfaces. The Seven Mile's bake config (`tools/gis/networks/osm-keys-seven-mile.json`) has the island and the posts; re-baked with `tbgis network`, and of the 14 road files only `osm-sm-bridge.json` (six posts), `osm-sm-little-duck-key.json` (one) and `osm-sm-old-east.json` (the island) changed, plus the network's notes; the sources' timestamps and hashes were left as committed (a re-bake moves them and nothing else).

Not done, on purpose: Bahia Honda, Spanish Harbor and Big Pine are a stretch bake (`tbgis bake`), which compresses long straights, so a mile there is not 1,609 m of game road and it has no anchor to project from; their posts need their own decision. The Key West roads and the hand-made `keys-m1` have none either. The island has no surf, no ground fog and no archive of the camp's buildings beyond the kit's two cottages (the article lists eleven). Tests: `src/render/pigeon-key.test.ts` (new: the island inside its box, more than 10 m clear of every road (20.5 m measured), its cottages on its grass, the dock from land to water, boats clear of it with a control that floats 46 near it without the rule), `src/render/mile-posts.test.ts` (new: a mile apart and falling by one, mile 46 at 0.804 of a mile into the bridge, on the right, none on the old road, facing the rider, each painting its own number, one cut blanking all), the `Milepost` and `Landmark` cases in `tools/gis/tests/test_capabilities.py`, a Pigeon Key case in `tools/gis/routes-keys-pt3.test.ts`, and the new params in `src/road/validate-gap.test.ts` and `feature-params.test.ts`. Also run: the landmark, text-surface, bridge-bay and Keys scenery tests, `tools/gis/region-routes.test.ts` and the Seven Mile sim tests. Not covered: how it looks on the phone (done, not phone-verified); no browser was run.
