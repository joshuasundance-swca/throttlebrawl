The riders are real people now, on real bikes. Every rival wears their own costume on their own machine: Deacon's duster streams off a chopper, Tammy's beehive rides a dirt bike with a cooler strapped on, Kevin's tie flies over his shoulder on a step-through, Mother Rust leans into a bagger, the Mayor flies a VOTE sign off his touring bike, the cops ride police motorcycles and Officer Meter his parking trike, and the Pacific Northwest and San Francisco locals have theirs. They pop a wheelie off the line, stand the bike on its nose under hard braking, squat on a landing, throw visible punches and kicks, swing weapons, flail in a crash and run back to a bike leaning on its stand. A rider who's taken a beating loses their hat (or phone, or cooler), and a badly hurt one trails smoke. Chad films himself, the Mayor waves, Gus rings his bell. The models load with the race, region by region; until they arrive you see the old block riders.
