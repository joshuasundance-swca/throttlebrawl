On Chuckanut Drive the mist now lies longer and lighter, so the bay below the cliff shows as water instead of a pale band; the rest of the Pacific Northwest keeps its misty mood, Bridge City and I-5 included. By Lake Samish, the lake beside East Shore Drive is water you can see from the usual camera, with its docks against it, where it was a pale band beyond the cabins.

For devs (the maintainer's playtest 4 answers, 2026-10-06: "Thin it on Chuckanut: a longer, lighter haze on that drive only, so the sea below the cliff shows. The rest of the region keeps its misty mood."; and run C's fix check, punch item 5).

- **The haze per stretch is data.** A road tagged `thin-haze` takes `render.thinHazeFarM` (a new slider, 700 m: the classic look's own fog end, the most the camera's 760 m far plane leaves room for) in a foggy region, where the region's is `render.regionFogFarM` (480 m). Chuckanut Drive's three roads carry it all along, and so does East Shore Drive. `src/render/haze.ts` reads the tag where the player is each frame and eases the fog's end toward it (1 s time constant), so a turn-off opens the view over a second or two. The tag is in the four baked road files (added by hand, not re-baked), the two bake configs' `tags`, and the GIS lint's tag list.
- **The lake took the wrong haze law.** As a backdrop floor above the sea it was hazed by straight distance from 0 m, per vertex, while three's fog on the land round it starts at 220 m and goes by view depth: at 150 m the lake was 0.23 haze beside a clear bank. It now takes three's own law per pixel (`backdrop/builder.ts`: the fragment shader's `smoothstep(uFogNear, uFogFar, vDepth)`, the backdrop gets the fog's start each frame; `floorHazeAt` mirrors it, and with no fog start it is the old law). Lake Samish is the only lake in any backdrop, so nothing else changes.
- **Tests.** `src/render/haze.test.ts`: every 10 m of every PNW road reads the haze its tag says (1,186 stations thin, 3,147 the region's); Bridge City and I-5's interstate roads keep 480 m; the ease never jumps or overshoots and is 95 % there in 3 s. `src/render/backdrop/lake-haze.test.ts`: the mirror equals three's smoothstep by view depth, the old law (the control) hazed the lake at 150 m, and the shader carries the new line and uniform. `tests/sim/pnw-water-sight.test.ts` (new, with `water-sight.test-util.ts`): from the settled chase camera on the phone's 915 by 412 screen, rays every 14 px meet the road scene and the backdrop's floors, and each water pixel's haze is the renderer's, mirrored. Chuckanut, 27 stations along the bay-side drop: 42 % of the bay pixels under half haze, 27 % with 480 m (the control). East Shore Drive s 900 to 2700, 13 stations: 89 % of the lake pixels under half haze; 37 % with the old law and 480 m (the control), 56 % with the old law and 700 m, 79 % with the new law and 480 m. Docks, three seeds, the chase camera 50 to 20 m before each: 47 of 49 in frame, at least two of four deck points in sight over the bank, at least 40 px wide, against water under 0.1 haze; with the old law and 480 m the water behind 44 of them was 0.2 haze or more.
- **The brief's first-sight test does not separate the two.** It asked that the bay be less than half fogged at its first sight with a control. Three's fog goes by view depth, and at 22 of the 27 stations the nearest bay pixel is nearer than the fog's 220 m start, so the first-seen water was never fogged, with either haze: the earlier estimate (first sight 206 to 382 m out) was a straight distance. What the 480 m haze took was the rest of the band behind it, so the test holds the share of the bay that reads instead, and prints the first-sight depths.
- **Budgets.** No geometry changes: draw calls and triangles per route are as before (the haze culls nothing; the far plane is unchanged). First load: one small module in the main chunk (see the report's measurement).
