Two new real-road races drawn from real road networks, each with a four-lane highway and a real junction choice. In the Keys, "Key West" runs the Overseas Highway four lanes wide over Stock Island and Cow Key Channel into Key West, then South Roosevelt Boulevard along Smathers Beach; at the Triangle you can keep right onto North Roosevelt Boulevard, the strip-mall boulevard (four lanes in the game, empty for the race), which comes back down 1st and Bertha Streets about 265 m shorter. In the Pacific Northwest, "I-5 by Lake Samish" races Interstate 5 over the Chuckanut Mountains, four lanes and a grass median with traffic to split; at exit 246 you can keep right off the interstate and take the lake's north and east shore roads instead, about 250 m longer and no traffic, and back on at the Nulle Road on-ramp. Signs at both splits say which way. Both are in the "Which road" picker, and the career races them: Chad Speedwell's grudge match now rides Key West, and Deputy Lindqvist's cop escape now runs on I-5. Done, not phone-verified.

For developers: `tools/gis` gained network bakes (`uv run tbgis network networks/<id>.json`). A network config names several real paths ("lines") and the junction choices between them; each line is pinned back onto the real map at every junction it meets (the bake's smoothed line drifted 0.4 to 43 m off the real junctions on these two networks, and about 6 m per city block before), and the bake writes the junction roads, split zones and named branches the hand-made tracks use. Key West's four-lane beach road narrows to two lanes at Bertha Street in the race direction, the first lane drop a racer meets (the W-R funnel eases riders over).
