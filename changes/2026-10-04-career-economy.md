Money is tighter, so each new bike is something you save for. A crash now costs a repair bill (1% of the bike's price, at least $50 a wreck, up to five wrecks, and never more than a quarter of what the race paid), shown on the results screen as "Repairs ×3". Replaying a race you already won pays half its prize and its required bonus (style cash and optional bonuses pay in full), so going back to an easy race is no way to get rich. The producer's ask and the side gig pay a little more at each tier up the career: a $250 ask pays $350 at the fifth tier and $500 at the twelfth. Wheelie and drift cash show on the ledger as "Wheelies" and "Drifts". The purses and bike prices themselves change with the content tasks that rewrite the career maps; this change is the rules they pay under. Not phone-verified.

For devs: `src/career/settle.ts` gains `repairBill`, `bikePrice`, `seasonPurseScale` (Season 2 pays 1.15 times the purse, Season 3 on 1.3) and the `REPAIR_*` and `REPLAY_PAY_SCALE` constants; `settleRace` reports `repairs` and `replay` (the bike defaults to the profile's current bike, or pass `bike`). `RaceTally.wrecks` (optional) counts the player's falls once each in `race-log.ts`. A `style-cash` objective with `params.kinds` counts only those kinds and reads `DRIFT $x/$y` for `["drift"]`. `ShowText.tierBase` and `askCashAt` in `show.ts` scale the ask and the gig, so the prompt and the ledger agree with no change in app/. Still to wire in app/ (the app-wiring task): the near-miss, airtime, oncoming and steal rewards that grow 9% a tier, and the global tier for anything else that needs it. `src/career/economy.test.ts` guards the rules.
