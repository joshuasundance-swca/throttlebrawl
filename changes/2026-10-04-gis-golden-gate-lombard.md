Two real San Francisco roads are on the route picker now. **Golden Gate** starts on the top of Hawk Hill, winds down Conzelman Road's hairpins, joins the highway at Vista Point and crosses the bridge, six lanes wide and about 67 m over the water, to the toll plaza: 6.0 km in all, 190 m of it downhill, the deck level and without a dip. **Lombard Street** starts at Polk, climbs to the crest at Hyde and drops straight into the crooked block, its eight real hairpins in red brick and one lane wide, then runs on to Telegraph Hill and up to the circle at the tower: 1.8 km, with grades up to 22%. The bridge has no towers or cables yet, and the tower on Telegraph Hill is not drawn yet either: both come with the art in the next wave. The bridge's railings keep you on it, and nothing in the game makes a joke of the water under it. Not phone-verified.

For devs: both are `tbgis network` bakes of OpenStreetMap and USGS 3DEP, committed (`tools/gis/networks/osm-sf-golden-gate.json` and `osm-sf-lombard.json`, their `osm-sf-gg-*` and `osm-sf-lombard-*` files in `region-sf`, a backdrop network file each, and the reports in `tools/gis/reports/`). They are 56.0 KB and 31.7 KB of gzip, fetched with the San Francisco pack (not first load; about 254 KB of JSON raw against the 15 MB download limit, not built locally). The Golden Gate is one `golden-gate#gg_bridge` landmark feature anchorage to anchorage on the bridge road, and Coit Tower one `sf-landmarks#coit_tower`: both draw nothing until CX3's kits land. The backdrop's far `golden-gate-bridge` piece is untouched, as the plan's gate G2 says, but its own keep-out hides all 18 of its slabs on this route (measured against the baked road), so until the kit is placed the bridge here is the deck, the railing and the sea. Both bots finish both routes (3:31 and 1:23 at seed 1), and the busiest view is 61 draw calls and 86k triangles on the Golden Gate, 45 and 50k on Lombard (the caps are 80 and 110k).

The bake gained five switches, each off by default (the Russian Hill network and the Twin Peaks stretch bake to byte-identical files with the old code and the new, on one cached extract): `bridgeWays` (a way the map leaves untagged that still carries the deck), `elevation.waterBridgeMinM` (open water under a long span deck), `crossSection.oneWay` (one forward lane, so a 5 m hairpin passes the road format's width rule), `roadSurface` (brick for one road) and `roads[].realName` (the picker shows "Golden Gate", not OSM's name). New: `tools/gis/tests/test_real_places.py` (8 pytest tests, all red against the old code, which lacks the switches) and `tools/gis/routes-sf-pt3.test.ts` (11 vitest tests: the bridge's lanes, height, railing and landmark, Lombard's eight hairpins and one lane, a negative control that a two-way table fails the lint, the crest speeds). Two shared tests changed with the roads: `tools/gis/region-routes.test.ts` lists the two networks (and lets a one-way road have one lane), and `tools/road/lane-drops-live.test.ts` lists the three lane drops the new roads add, on purpose. Each pad slot has two candidates, as `tools/packs/reachability.test.ts` wants. Touched `tools/gis/README.md` and `docs/content-packs.md` (the one-way section, the landmark bakes, the picker's list). `region.json` only gains the two network ids.
