Bridge City now looks like downtown Portland out to the horizon, and the blocks are thicker. Ride the Burnside Bridge and downtown stands ahead over the river: a tall pink tower with two setbacks, a tower with a sloped crown, one with a pyramid on top, glass towers round South Waterfront and the Lloyd district, and low brick blocks on both banks. Across the river stand the other bridges with their traffic crawling over them: the Steel Bridge with its two lift towers, the red Broadway Bridge, the double-deck Marquam, the white Tilikum Crossing with its fan of cables, the Ross Island, Fremont's arch and St. Johns' green towers. The West Hills fill the sky behind downtown with their broadcast mast, Mount Tabor, Rocky Butte and Mount Scott stand to the east, and a tug works the Willamette. Up close, the streets are two buildings deep now: offices, brick lofts and tall pink towers stand behind the street fronts, and each cross street runs on between them to a small cast-iron front at its far end.

For devs (playtest 4, P4-20: "Bridge City should look like downtown Portland even in the distance etc and be as content-rich as the others"; builds on CX4's kit, #506, and T12.6's blocks, #508).

- **Wider land for the blocks** (`scenery.ts` `WIDE_LAND_M`, `road-mesh.ts`). The `blocks` theme's land strip is 60 m past the verge, tried first; where another road is in the way it falls back to 42 m, the usual 24, 14 and 6. Every other theme keeps its 24 m: `portland-blocks.test.ts` shows the same roads with the `pdx-blocks` tag taken off keep 24 m at most, and 97 % of three roads' samples have 56 m or more with it. A small shared-config edit in the road scene, as AGENTS.md allows when only this change needs it. The per-PR land geometry (`tests/sim/geometry-land.test.ts` on `osm-pnw-portland`: 0 open, 0 slivers, 0 wet, 0 under land) and scenery sweeps (`geometry-scenery-named`) stay green with their known lists empty.
- **The second row** (`downtown.ts` `planPortland`). After every road's street fronts are placed (so a second row never takes a front's lot), offices, brick lofts and pink towers (60 to 150 m, stacked from the modules, never stretched) stand at least `PDX_BACK_ROW_M` (31.2 m: a sidewalk, the deepest front and an alley) past the verge, behind about 70 % of the metres the fronts cover, taller on average than the fronts (about 47 m against 30 m). Cross streets run on between the rows, closed at the land's edge by a small cast-iron front (`pdx-end`, 60 of them, each corridor tested free). Each building keeps every rule the fronts keep and one more: it stands on no other road's land at a different height (`offOtherLand`). New tests in `src/render/portland-blocks.test.ts` hold the width, the second row, its height, the street ends, and the no-overlap and on-land rules for four seeds; the existing land, overlap, feature and ground tests now cover the second row too.
- **Density against San Francisco's downtown**, as buildings per 100 m of avenue on land (the same count on both: towers, back towers, plaza towers, cross-street buildings and headquarters on San Francisco's): Bridge City went from 6.6 (250 buildings) to 12.4 (473), San Francisco's is 14.3 (530). So 87 % of it, not all: San Francisco's cross streets run 120 m with their own buildings, queued cross traffic and lamps, where this second row ends at the strip and CX4's kit has no lamp. Not done: cross traffic and signals on Bridge City's cross streets, lamps, a third row.
- **The horizon** (`packs/region-pnw/assets/backdrop/pacific-northwest/region.json`, 23 new pieces, all `networks: ["osm-pnw-portland"]`): five skylines (the West End with four authored towers, Old Town and the Pearl, Lloyd with two glass spires, South Waterfront, and the hospital hill 125 m up), two low-city blocks pieces, seven bridges (`lift`, two `truss`, `stayed`, `girder`, `arch`, `suspension`; the route's own Burnside, Morrison and Hawthorne are the road itself), the West Hills ridge with its mast, three buttes, a tug, the Willamette and the Columbia as water floors and an urban land floor. Hood, St. Helens and Adams were already the region's. Every standing piece keeps 160 to 200 m off every road, inside the geometry sweep's 150 m (`tests/sim/geometry-backdrop.test.ts` on `osm-pnw-portland`: closest standing vertex 180 m, 0 of 174 cameras x 3 fog ends see a floor over the sea). So the downtown inside the route's loop is the near blocks' and the skylines stand round it. The towers are invented silhouettes in the manner of the city's best known ones, no building copied, no brand or text. No other Pacific Northwest road draws any of it: the Columbia gorge's own walls stand between it and the city, so none of the other routes sees Portland.
- **Two new backdrop features** (`backdrop/data.ts`, `shapes.ts`; `docs/content-packs.md`, Backdrop): a skyline can place authored `towers` each with a `crown` (`flat`, `stepped`, `pyramid`, `slant`, `spire`), a `gridDeg` and a ground `baseM` (the seeded scatter draws the same numbers as before, so San Francisco's skyline is unchanged; a test builds a skyline with and without the new fields set empty and gets the same triangles), and a `stayed` bridge style (towers with stays fanned down to the deck, none over a gap). New tests: `backdrop/skyline-towers.test.ts` and the `stayed` block in `bridge-styles.test.ts`.
- **What a rider sees** (`backdrop/portland.test.ts`, each with a control built without the Portland pieces): from the Burnside Bridge, Broadway and the Hawthorne Bridge the city stands over 35, 24 and 25 % of the forward 70 degrees at 1.5 degrees or more (0 % without), up to 6.5 degrees; the Steel Bridge's lift towers rise to 6.2 degrees over its deck from the Burnside Bridge; the West Hills cover the western 80 degrees from Broadway up to 6 degrees; the Willamette lies under the Burnside Bridge and nowhere along Broadway or East Burnside. `backdrop.test.ts` lists `osm-pnw-portland` among the networks with signature pieces and movers.
- **Cost, the busiest Bridge City view against the caps** (`scene-cost.test.ts`): `osm-pnw-portland-morrison-in` at s 35, a junction connector with 12 of its draws downtown stretches: 70 draw calls and 79,380 triangles, up from 70 and 64,467 (the downtown stretches there now draw 28,574 of them). The backdrop adds one draw call and 12,843 triangles (Bridge City's, seed 7; the backdrop test's cap is 20,000), so the busiest view is about 71 draw calls and 92,200 triangles against the still scene's share of 80 and 110,000 and the frame's caps of 120 and 150,000. Frame time and the first-load JavaScript are in the PR's checks; the backdrop data is a lazy chunk and the road scene's change is a few lines.
- **Not done, not checked.** No browser was run: the look (colours, the squeeze depth of a 160 m tower at 250 m, the grey lot paving behind the second row, a cast-iron front closing each street) is checked by geometry and by the views above, not by eye, and is not phone-verified. The authored positions and bridge coordinates are approximate (map positions to about 100 m, sized "as it reads from the road"). Randomly scattered towers inside a skyline's radius are culled near a road, so the West End's scatter is thinner on the road side.
