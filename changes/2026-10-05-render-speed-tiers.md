The game now keeps itself smooth when a phone struggles. When frames start running late, the 3D picture gets slightly softer, in small steps, until the frame rate holds; when there is room again it sharpens back, more slowly, so it doesn't flicker between the two. It never drops below a readable floor, and the speedometer, buttons and text are never softened. If softening alone isn't enough, the game moves down a quality tier, which draws the distant scenery a little closer in. It never touches traffic, riders, cops or hazards, so nothing that can hit you is hidden. The game remembers the tier that suited your device for next time. A new **Graphics** row in Display settings lets you pin High, Medium or Low instead of Auto. Not tested on the benchmark phone yet.

For devs: `src/render/quality.ts` (new, pure) holds the tiers as data, the readable floor (0.75 device pixels per CSS pixel), `renderPixelRatio`, `sceneryReach`, the governor (`createQualityGovernor`) and the per-device auto tier (`<prefix>:quality`). app/ feeds the governor each drawn frame's interval in the loop's render and passes its answer to the new `GameRenderer.setQuality`. That sets the WebGL pixel ratio (the browser upscales, so the classic look pays no extra pass) and trims the still scenery's draw and far-detail distances. Boards keep their full reach. The budget is the display's measured interval times the frame-rate cap's divisor (smooth first: a 90 Hz panel aims for 90 fps). The resolution steps down 0.1 per half-second window over 1.2 budgets and up 0.05 after four windows with room. A missed rise goes back and waits twice as long, up to 30 s. `auto` drops a tier after four over-budget windows at the tier's floor. It climbs after 10 s with room, a wait that doubles after each drop. Under the test flag all of it is off unless a spec sets `window.__dynamicResolution = true`, so the perf check and the other specs still draw `high` at full resolution, with unchanged draw calls and triangles. Settings: `qualityTier` (`auto` by default, additive, version 1) and the Graphics row on the Display tab. These touch save/, ui/ and app/ outside the render lane, because the pin is a setting. Docs: architecture.md "Quality tiers and dynamic resolution" (rewritten to what was built) and its testing seams, engineering.md's CI rendering and perf-check lines (the test flag now leaves the tiers off rather than pinning `low`), and a product-spec settings row. All numbers are `[default]`.

Tests: `src/render/quality.test.ts` drives the governor frame by frame through a device model (work rounded up to whole display refreshes). It covers: a quick step down, then a tier drop, never under the floor; a slower climb back; settling near what a device can hold, with at most 6 changes in a minute on a device that fits at 0.85; a lone 250 ms hitch counting as no load; nothing judged outside a race or in its grace; the budget following the frame-rate cap and a 90 Hz panel; a pinned tier holding; `auto` starting from and climbing back from its stored tier; lower tiers never costing more; every tier's scenery reaching past `MIN_THREAT_DRAW_M`; and the stored tier surviving bad storage. Three of these were checked to fail under a matching break (no retry back-off, no hitch clamp, judging non-race frames). `src/ui/settings.test.ts` checks the Graphics row and `src/save/save-m2.test.ts` the new field. `tests/e2e/render-quality.spec.ts` (new) checks, through the real loop and renderer with forced 60 ms frames, that Auto ends on `low` with a lower pixel ratio at or above the floor, kept across a reload, and that a pinned `high` holds while the resolution reaches 0.75. `tests/e2e/ui-settings.spec.ts` gets the Graphics probe. The browser specs were not run on the dev machine; CI runs them. Not measured: the benchmark phone's frame time on any tier, and whether 0.75 device pixels per CSS pixel reads well there. Not phone-verified.
