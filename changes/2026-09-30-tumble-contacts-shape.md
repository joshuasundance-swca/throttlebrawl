tumble-2 replaces the crude two-body tumble with a point-mass rig (a three-point ragdoll rider, a rigid four-point bike) and adds its contacts, the rail and splash, and the rival get-up. The architecture doc's crash tumble section gains one `[default]` bullet with the event shapes other lanes read: a body touching a car is a `crash` with `data.contact` `tumble` whose actor is already down (count it as a contact, not a rider going down); a body knocking a rider off is a new `crash` on the same causeId; `getUp` carries `data.crashTick`, and `respawn` carries `data.crashTick` and `data.splashTick`. Three new sliders under "crash": splash penalty (4 s), rival get-up (1.5 s) and the speed at which a flying body knocks a rider off (12 m/s). The snapshot's `tumble` bodies are now the centres of the two clusters. One fix outside the lane, in the open: the resume test's tampered checkpoint (`hash ^ 0`) turned any state hash above 2^31 negative, so it reported a desync at tick 0 whenever the state happened to hash that high; this PR's new state did, so the test now keeps the hash unsigned (`>>> 0`). A second: the real-road test (tools/gis) now races the field without the cop, because on some seeds a crash that this PR moves lands near him and the bust ends the bot's race before the finish; the test proves the road, not the cop. Two M1 tumble tests adjust for the new behaviour: a rival someone knocked off now takes the 1.5 s get-up before running, and a bike can stop right beside its rider.
