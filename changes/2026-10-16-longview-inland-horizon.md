Longview's street courses and Port Shift now look into an inland valley: long industrial roofs,
narrow mill-stack silhouettes, a modest civic roofline and wooded hills in the middle distance.
The roofs and stacks use stronger muted brick and steel colors and closer, modestly enlarged forms
so they can read above the distant haze.
These AI-authored composites give the rides local context; they are original scenery, not mapped
buildings or surveyed landmark positions. They stay well beyond the roadside ground and add no
physical obstacles or changes to the roads.

The Portland isolation test now compares each other course's built scenery with and without the
Portland pieces, preserving the no-leak guarantee while allowing those courses their own skylines
and bridges. A positive control still requires Portland's geometry to change when its pieces are
removed. This replaces the old assumption that every other course has zero skylines or bridges.
