Holding right all the way onto the old Seven Mile Bridge's ramp truck no longer wears your speed away (playtest 4 run A's leftover: the maintainer, "it's just too hard to get on").

- **The platform's edge guides you.** On the short road leading to the first ramp truck and on the repair platforms themselves, a bike held against the water's edge now slides along it. Before, the water slowed it hard (10 m/s every second), so a rider who kept the stick right after the turn-off reached the truck far too slow.
- It is the same rule the turn-off's own zone already used, for a road built for a hop: any road with a ramp truck and a gap just past its lip, and the short connector onto it.

What was measured (one rider alone on the real road, at each bike's top speed, holding right the whole way from three places across the turn-off):

- Before: the Chopper and the Rustbucket missed all 3 starts (6 of 6) and the Sport 600 missed 2 of 3. Each of those reached the truck 15 to 22 m/s slower than it entered.
- Now: all 21 starts (the 7 bikes at least as fast as the Rustbucket) reach the old road, and every bike reaches the truck within 2 m/s of the speed it entered with.
- A control: the same hold on the old bridge itself still scrapes (5.7 m/s lost in two seconds), so the check can see an edge slowing a bike.
- The way back onto the highway was also run holding right and passes, with or without the change.

Not changed: the old bridge's own edges, and every other road, still scrape as before.
