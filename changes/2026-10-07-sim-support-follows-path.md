Standing still on a truck's roof round a bend now keeps you where you are on it. Before, braking to a stop on a semi on the I-5 bend by Lake Samish slid you sideways about 25 cm a second until you dropped off its side. And a truck you have just ridden off is solid again at once: dropping off its side or rolling off its back you fall clear of it, and if you steer back into it you bump it like any other truck. Before, you could fall straight through the trailer and vanish inside it, and the bike's nose could sink into a semi's tail as you slid off its back. Not phone-verified.

For devs: the live check of 2026-10-07 (mustFix 1 and 2, and punch item 1), under the maintainer's rule of 2026-10-06 (`[decided]`: one consistent physics; what is drawn is what is met, nothing is a ghost). `src/sim/riders/supports.ts`: a traffic vehicle's top carries its rider along the vehicle's own path (`vehiclePath`, used by `vehicleSupports`). Its middle moves at its speed in metres of s a second at its lane's offset, so its world velocity along the road is shortened off the middle of a bend by `sRateFactor`. Its box turns with the road under its middle (curvature times speed), so the support's velocity is the velocity of the point the rider stands on, and the box `supportAt` predicts `ahead` seconds on turns too. A moving ramp truck's top is carried at its speed along the road where the rider is (`sRateFactor` at his offset). On a straight road all of this is exactly what it was. `clearOfVehicleTop` is gone: a rider leaving a vehicle's top no longer writes traffic's `launchedOff`, so traffic's `contacts` meets him at once by its rule at a top's edge from #670. Leaving the top, he slides clear of it the short way: 0.43 m off a semi's side, 1.02 m off its back, in the tick he leaves. Coming back at it, he meets it by the closing speed.
