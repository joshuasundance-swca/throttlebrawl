The world no longer ends at the verge. Every region now has a horizon: in the Pacific Northwest, the Olympics and the Cascades with snow-capped volcanoes, clear-cut patchwork on the nearer hills, a ferry crossing, and on the Columbia River Highway the gorge walls with waterfalls, the river and a tug pushing a barge; in San Francisco, an invented downtown skyline (one tower wears a large glowing orb for no stated reason), the two big bridges, Twin Peaks and the striped broadcast mast, the bay with freighters, sailboats and a ferry, and a fog bank that slowly rolls in; in the Keys, long low bridges (the old one has a span missing), mangrove islands, reef lights, sailboats and thunderheads on the horizon. The clouds, boats, islands, clear-cuts and waterfalls change from race to race. It all draws in one pass, loads only when you race in that region, and works in every look.
