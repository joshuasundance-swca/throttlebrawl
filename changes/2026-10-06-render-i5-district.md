Interstate 5 over the Chuckanut Mountains now reads as an interstate, not a country road. It has a wide paved shoulder ending in a steel guard rail, concrete parapets on its two bridges, big green guide signs with their own words (Lake Samish's exit, Seattle or Canada, the Nulle Road on-ramp, the weigh station), and semis in the traffic. The mailboxes, firewood stacks, espresso huts and split-rail fences that used to stand on its verge are gone. The ferns and trees stay.

One thing plays differently: the old verge was 6 m of dirt and ferns that slowed you down, and the shoulder is now 3 m of paving past the lanes ending in a wall, so a rider who runs out of shoulder meets a rail, as on any other highway in the game. The pullout at the summit and the billboards moved out behind the rail.

For devs: `[default]`, not phone-verified, no browser run. The details are in `docs/content-packs.md` ("Barrier looks", "I-5 as an interstate, as built").
- **Barrier looks are a seam** (the sheets' shared seam for the Gorge's stone walls, Chuckanut's parapet and this): `src/render/barrier-looks.ts` is a table of looks, `railing`, `concrete` (a 0.81 m New Jersey profile) and `guardrail` (a W-beam, 0.76 m), each a panel with its length, height, stand-off and draw distance. A barrier takes its own `look`, else its road tag's (`BARRIER_LOOK_BY_TAG`: on `interstate`, a `rail` is a guard rail and a `wall` is concrete), and a hard verge edge on a tagged side draws the tag's `edge` look at the band's edge, left out within 6 m of a branch ramp. `verge.ts` draws one instanced mesh per look in use (the railing is the same code, same numbers, same mesh name); `road-mesh.ts` leaves the solid band of any barrier with a look out. The look word is in `BARRIER_LOOKS` and in the GIS config's `BarrierLook`, and `tools/gis/barrier-looks-vocabulary.test.ts` holds the road format, the bake and the table to one list.
- **The district**: a new tag, `interstate`, on I-5's three roads and the two junction pieces (and over each bridge deck via `deckTags`); `VERGE_BY_TAG` gives it a 3 m `shoulder` band ending `hard`, first in the list; the Pacific Northwest kit's mailbox, firewood, espresso (both), sign, split-rail and log-fence rules take `notDistrict: ['interstate']`; the bake's `bridgeBarrier` is a 0.81 m `wall` with the `concrete` look (it was a 1 m rail, which let a tumbling body over, to the sea 150 m below). No OSM extract is in the repo, so the five baked roads were edited by hand to what the config now says, and `tools/gis/routes-pnw-i5.test.ts` holds the two to each other.
- **Signs**: a `guide` sign is a bigger board (`GUIDE_SIGN_SIZE`: a 2.4 m panel on 2.6 m legs, 4.6 to 6.4 m wide). The five guide slots name their own region sign (four new, vetoable: `i5-exit-246`, `i5-seattle-canada`, `i5-exit-242`, `i5-weigh-station`; none draws from the pool any more, which could print "BIGFOOT CROSSING" on a green guide sign), at d 15.5 to 20.5, outside the rail. The three billboards moved to d -27 to -19 and the summit pullout to d 16 to 22.
- **Traffic**: `region-pnw:semi` (16 by 2.6 m, as long as the log truck so the rival AI's largest-vehicle size does not change; the shared semi model, now wired, so its waiver in `vehicles.test.ts` is gone) in a new `interstate` traffic area with cars, pickups, wagons and the odd camper; no cyclists, hay trucks or espresso stands in tow there.
- **Cost**: I-5 route views, main vs this branch: draw calls max 59 to 60 (the limit is 120), triangles max 100,490 to 99,322, from `scene-cost.test.ts`; the interstate pool is 8 draw calls with models (+2 over boxes), from `draw-headroom.test.ts`; first-load JavaScript 479,383 to 480,408 bytes gzip (+1,025) of 512,000, so 31,592 headroom, from a local `vite build` of each.
- **Not built, on purpose**: a concrete median barrier. The median is a 4 m gap a rider can ride across, so a barrier drawn there would be an invisible one, and a solid one is a sim change (riders' limits by side, and it ends the oncoming lanes as a hazard on this route), which is the maintainer's call. Also not built: rumble strips, overhead gantries, the weigh station itself (sheet I4) and Lake Samish (I3).
