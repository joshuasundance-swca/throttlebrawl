Riders no longer ride through each other. Bikes that touch are pushed apart. A side bump shoves both riders sideways and makes them wobble, so you can bump a rival toward traffic or the shoulder. Running into someone's back merges your speeds with no bounce and glances you both aside, so you slide past rather than get stuck behind them. The shoulder stays slower than the road: flat out, you top out at about 80 % of your lane speed there.
