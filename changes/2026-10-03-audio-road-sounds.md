The road events and the smashables now make noise, and the rivals talk about them. A smashed prop sounds like what it was: a parking meter clangs and spills its coins, a mailbox dents and scatters letters, a cafe table takes its crockery with it, lobster traps crunch, and a firewood stand splinters and drops the honour box. The boat trailer's coupler clanks and the hull scrapes off down the causeway; the log truck's chains let go and the logs rumble off; a wheel over a log thumps; the lane-vote gantry flips with a ka-chunk. In San Francisco the runaway cable car's grip lets go with a squeal, and its bell rings in a hurry the whole time it rolls back at you. A thrown briefcase whirls and bursts with paperwork, landing on a rider is a body slam, and a landing that surges whooshes. A rival past half health has an engine that misfires and backfires, more often the closer he is to going down. Your finish is a boxing bell struck three times; a rival crossing the line gets one ding. The rivals have 18 new lines for the boat slide, the gator crossing, the log spill, the cable-car runaway and the three lane votes ("We're rolling back. It's a feature."). Each line can be cut like any other.
