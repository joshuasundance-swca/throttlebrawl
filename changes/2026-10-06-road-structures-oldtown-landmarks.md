Key West's Old Town street fronts and every landmark are now the road's structure plan, the physical world's next step (the maintainer, 2026-10-06: "consistent physics and gameplay is important here so players know what to expect and how to interact with the world"; "Yeah that sounds good :)"). The picture does not change; the sim does not read the plan yet.

- `src/road/structures/oldtown.ts` (layer `oldtown`): where Duval's and Whitehead's shopfronts, open bars and second row of conch houses stand, render's street-front rule moved unchanged. It reads the model boxes from the contract's table and the drawn land from the road's own rule (`src/road/land.ts`), never what has loaded. Each building is its parts at their drawn shapes: the body up to a pitched roof (a hipped end in slices), the balcony and its posts over the sidewalk (the sidewalk under it stays open), a porch with its roof and steps, and an open bar's walls, roof, counter and café tables.
- `src/road/structures/landmarks.ts` (layer `landmarks`) with `landmark-places.ts` (render's placement, moved unchanged) and `landmark-parts.ts` (generated from the committed files): every landmark's solid parts at their drawn heights. A building is solid from its ground to its roofs; a gate, a truss or a lift tower is solid in each column from its lowest drawn point to its highest; what lies under a bridge's deck is left out (under the course). The Golden Gate's towers, anchorages, main cables and suspender ropes, Fred the Tree, Pigeon Key, the Twin Peaks lot (sheared to the road's grade) and the cruise ship's top deck are built from render's own figures. A rain cloud is weather, not a solid.
- Render draws the plan: the roadside scatter stands the plan's fronts (`planOldTown`) and everything else it places keeps off their ground; the landmark layer draws each node where the plan places it. Both import their planner directly (the module map's `structures/*` entry), so it stays in the lazy `road-structures` chunk.
- What changed in the picture, and why: at roadside density 0 Old Town's buildings now stand (they are solids the sim will meet; the debug slider used to drop them), and before the landmark kits load the fronts already keep off the landmarks' ground (render used to place them once without it, then again when the kits arrived). Against main's own scatter on the six seeds the tests use, every one of Duval's 8,794 roadside props stands where main stood it, but two fronts of seed 3 that moved less than a millimetre (the model box's float rounding).

Tests (each with a negative control): `scripts/structures-oldtown.test.ts` holds the plan to what main drew (`src/render/structures.golden.json`, written by main's own scatter: 6 seeds, seed 7's 1,096 buildings each within 1 cm), shows render draws it at density 0 and 3 and with no drawn land, scenery or models, and holds every building's 14,806 structures to its drawn model (17.2 million points placed as render draws them, the farthest 0.27 m out); a structure moved half a metre is found. `scripts/structure-parts.test.ts` (the hitbox test's twin) holds Old Town's 8 models' parts to their files (0.4 m, every face within 0.3 m of the drawing) and measures the 1,517 landmark parts from the files again (within 1 cm). `scripts/structures-landmarks.test.ts` holds the 45 placements to main's on the 11 networks with landmarks, and every landmark's 2,529 structures to what the layer draws of it (23.8 million points). `scripts/structures-land.test.ts` holds the land rule to the drawn scene on 8 more networks, Duval among them (82,000 samples, none differ). `tests/sim/duval-sight.test.ts` builds its old second row (its control) through the plan's rule. First-load JavaScript 466.4 KB gzip, 30 B over main (33.6 KB headroom); the `road-structures` lazy chunk is 12.3 KB gzip. Not phone-verified.
