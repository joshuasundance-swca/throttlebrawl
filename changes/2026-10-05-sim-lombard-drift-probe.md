Playtest 3, T2.6 (the critic's C5): `tests/sim/drift-hairpins.test.ts` gains a Lombard case. A hairpin bot rides the baked Lombard route alone (the Rustbucket, Streetfighter and Superbike) and counts how many of the crooked block's nine hairpin apexes it passes in a drift. At the shipped floors (`riders.driftMinMps` 18, `riders.driftExitMps` 10 m/s) the slide holds through 1 to 3 of the nine and the bot crashes 3 to 4 times, because 18 m/s into a 5 m bend runs into the wall. At 12 / 6 it holds through 5 or 6 of the nine with no crash and 2 or 3 clean exits, and the block takes 1.5 to 2 s less than the same bot with the drift off. Lower pairs (10 / 5, 8 / 4, 6 / 3) hold through 6 to 9, but with up to 2 crashes. So a drift can hold on Lombard only with the two floors lowered, and the content has no way to lower them for one route: an event carries no tuning, so the floors are global, and 12 m/s for every bend of 150 m or less would start a drift in everyday braking. The drift also pays almost nothing there: $0 to $2 banked at $1 a drift second against Crown Point's $16, because a 12 m/s drift earns the 0.3 speed share and a barrier wobble empties the open meter. T10.6 therefore either gives a drift event its own floors and rate (a small contract change, its own PR) or ships Lombard as the slow "crooked mile" race. Also seen: from the climb's right-hand lane the first tick in the one-lane block is the block's wall (a wobble that cancels the first drift), so a start-side lane join would help any race on it. No game code changed; the test's printed `[T2.6]` table has the full sweep.
