The Pacific Northwest and San Francisco now look like places. In the Pacific Northwest, clusters of firs and cedars of every size line the forest roads and carry on into the distance, a sawmill with a teepee burner stands by the sawmill road, the trestle stands on timber bents, and a light drizzle falls in a grey, overcast light. In San Francisco, terraces of painted Victorian row houses line the streets and step down the hills, cable cars run on their line (with cable slots in the road), and fog banks lie out on the bay. In both, the land beside the road now slopes down to the ground instead of dropping into the sea, so no road floats in the void.
