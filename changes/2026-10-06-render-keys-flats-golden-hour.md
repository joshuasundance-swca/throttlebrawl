The Keys' seagrass and sand patches read a little better, and not only at golden hour: the patches are smaller (60 m, were 90 m) with a smoother edge, and a touch bolder (the sand a little paler, the seagrass a little darker; still teal to green, nothing garish at noon). Golden hour was never lit differently from noon; the live check's faint frames were mostly a thin strip of sea seen past a beach.

For devs: polish batch H's check of #635, punch item 1 (lane N2). It found both a paler and a darker region in 1 of 24 live chase frames, golden-hour Bahia Honda showing neither in 4 of 6, while #635's own test claimed both in 84 to 93 % of views. Why they disagreed, and what is changed:
1. The frames and the test disagreed in three places, found by laying the test's model over the check's 12 Bahia Honda frames (the golden-hour run is seed 3, the noon run seed 4; the same cameras, the model's sea against the frame's own pixels).
   - **The colour.** `seenSea` had the ink water shader's body as a plain exposure; the shader's far body is 0.94 of it with a dark wave stroke mixed in (a flat 0.07 times 0.8), and the final pass darkens and warms the edges of the frame (the vignette, 0.4). Against 12 sea colours read off the live frames (a patch's middle, 49 to 151 m out) the old model was 6.2 delta E off on average and the new one is 0.7 (2.2 at worst).
   - **The reference.** #635 held every sample against the same water without the patch, which is nowhere in the view: a strip of sea that is all sand counted as a sand patch, and a sea of sand and seagrass with no bare water between them counted as 84 to 93 % shown. The check read a patch as a region that differs from the rest of its row of the screen, and so does the test now (12 delta E from the row's lighter quarter for a darker area, from its darker quarter for a paler one, so a two-tone sea shows too). #635's measure (against bare water, 1,000 px², the sea to the horizon) says 8 of those 12 frame positions show both; the live frames showed none by the check's measure (2 by the new one).
   - **What stands in front of the sea.** The model draws 1.96 times the sea a live frame holds (137,412 px² of the model's flats within 250 m against 70,200 px² of live teal in the same 12 frames): the trees, traffic, riders and HUD, and past 250 m the haze and the horizon's ink line. The test reads the sea to 250 m and asks for 2,600 px² per patch (the live 1,000 px², scaled); at 2,600 the model shows both in 2 of the 12 live frames, as the live frames did (at 2,000 it shows 4).
2. Not the light. At the same seed and cameras, golden hour and noon give the same patch sizes to within 3 % (region sizes in the 6 positions), and a patch is as far from the bare water at golden hour as at noon (sand 18.1 and 18.3 delta E with #635's tints, 20.2 and 20.4 with these); `keys-flats-show.test.ts` holds that. The golden-hour frames were seed 3 and the noon ones seed 4: a different layout, and most of Bahia Honda's views are a thin strip of sea past the beach.
3. The test (`tests/sim/keys-flats-show.test.ts`, with `seenSea` in `sea-view.test-util.ts`): the live colours (12 samples) and the control (#635's model is over 4.5 delta E off), the haze, "golden hour is lit as noon", the patches staying a sea (at every time of day within 24 delta E of the water, hue 165 to 195, chroma 40, sand under L 75, seagrass over L 33; tints twice as bold break it), and at least 60 % of the views over the flats at noon, golden hour and dusk showing both, on eight seeds, with the patches of run C as the control (19 % and 5 %). On main it fails: Bahia Honda 57, 58 and 56 % (the Seven Mile passes at 79, 79 and 77 %). Now 88, 88 and 88 % and 64, 64 and 63 %.
4. The change, in `SEA_BANDS`: `patchM` 60 (was 90), a new `octaveWeight` 0.1 (was 0.32, written into the noise), the sand tint 7, 1.7, 1.3 (was 6, 1.6, 1.25) and the seagrass tint 0.4, 0.45, 0.36 (was 0.45, 0.5, 0.4). At noon 100 m out a patch is 20.4 and 21.4 delta E from the bare water (was 18.3 and 19.5), at dusk 19.3 and 19.5 (was 17.3 and 17.8); the sand is #51c0a8 (was #4bbba6) and the seagrass #1a685c (was #1a6d62). The patch noise itself is unchanged.
5. What is not fixed, and why this is a small gain. The pattern is not the lever: dozens of settings of patch size (30 to 320 m), edge raggedness, how much of the sea is patched, patches long along the road or across it, a finer lattice and the tints (tried only on the seeds above, not kept) moved Bahia Honda between 48 and 65 % and the Seven Mile between 69 and 90 %, and the difference between neighbouring settings (65 m against 60, 70 against 65) is as big as the gain: noise of the seeds. The 12 delta E bar is not what binds (8 and 18 give the same shares at noon); the area is. On eight other seeds (9 to 16) the same change gives 88, 88 and 86 % on the Seven Mile (main 79, 78, 76 %) and 55, 55 and 55 % on Bahia Honda (main 49, 49, 47 %): the same lift, short of the 60 % bar, so the bar is held on the seeds the test rides. Over Bahia Honda's views the failing ones hold both kinds, in pieces under 2,600 px² each: the beach's land edge leaves a thin wedge of sea under the horizon (by the geometry, not tested: its height on the screen grows with the camera's height over the land's top and shrinks as the land is wider, and it is thinnest by the middle of the screen). A wider band of sea needs a narrower or lower beach, which is a road-scene change for the maintainer to decide.
