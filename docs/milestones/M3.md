# Milestone 3: look

> **In plain words.** Milestone 3 gives the game a face. The four sample styles from the look lab (PS1 authentic, chunky low-poly, comic/cel and hazy painterly) come back, this time on the real game scene, and you can flip between them from the pause menu while you ride. On top of any of them you can lay a photocopied-zine overlay, and you can compare rider proportions (exaggerated or realistic) and colour palettes for each time of day. Agents also work on what makes the look *ours*: a signature palette, a real design for each of the eight rivals, the zine texture, and AI concept art to react to. The Keys start to look like somewhere: long bridges, water, mangroves, stilt houses, bait shacks, signs and billboards, plus gators, chickens and the odd boat parked in a lane. Menus turn into a 90s zine, the HUD and buttons become movable, and far-chase and helmet cameras arrive. The first 3D model built by a Blender script lands in the game. Your pick of look is a taste call; until you make it, a recommended look stays on and nothing waits.

## How to read this plan

- Tags and the task layout work as in [the M1 plan](./M1.md#how-to-read-this-plan). This plan is lighter than M2's: each task lists its goal, what it owns, what it builds, its automated acceptance, a phone check and its order. Exact numbers are left to the lanes unless a budget or a decision fixes them.
- *When* is owned by [the roadmap](../roadmap.md#m3--look). The look layer, the overlay seam and the budgets are owned by [the architecture doc](../architecture.md#look-layer-swappable).

## How the look test works without a gate

This follows [the roadmap](../roadmap.md#m3--look). [default]

- Agents build a **look switcher** on the pause menu. It flips the style, the overlay, the rider proportions and the palette live on the phone.
- The switcher ships with a **recommended default**, chosen by the lanes for readability at speed and frame rate on the phone probe.
- Work that doesn't depend on the style keeps going: scenery geometry, menu layout, the HUD editor and the art pipeline.
- When the maintainer picks, the pick becomes the default look id in the base pack: a data change. Until then the recommended default stays.
- Every look must keep rivals, the cop and traffic readable at speed, and must stay inside the frame budget. A look that misses the frame budget on the device is marked "slow" in the switcher rather than removed, so the maintainer can still see it. Readability at speed has no automated marker: it is the phone playtest's question, helped by the greyscale silhouette check on the eight rivals. [default]
- **What is gated, and for how long.** Until the maintainer picks, all four looks are gated: a smoke frame and the perf check with the full overlay, per look, plus the silhouette check. The overlay and time-of-day matrix runs on the recommended default look only. From the pick on, only the picked look stays in the gate; the others keep one smoke frame and are marked "unmaintained" (they may break visually without failing CI), so every later material kind, scenery item and view is not built and gated four times. Non-default looks are sketch quality. [default]

## Look and identity tasks

### look-1 · Four looks, the overlay and the palettes

- **Goal.** The four look-lab styles on the real scene, the zine overlay on top, and palettes per time of day. [decided for the look test and the overlay as an identity source; default for the rest]
- **Owns:** `src/render/` (except the rider views).
- **Builds:**
  - Four `LookStyle` implementations: PS1 authentic, chunky low-poly, comic/cel and hazy painterly, each requesting materials by kind ([the look layer](../architecture.md#look-layer-swappable)).
  - The `OverlayStyle` at off, subtle and full: halftone, toner grain, paper texture, registration offset and a torn-edge vignette, as the last stage of the one combined full-screen pass. [default]
  - Lighting presets for dawn, noon, golden hour, dusk and night, with each time of day able to carry its own palette ([the region format](../content-packs.md#region)).
  - Up to three **signature palette** candidates, fed by identity-1's proposals, selectable in the switcher. [decided that a signature palette is an identity source; default for "up to three"]
- **Automated acceptance:** every look renders a non-blank smoke frame in the browser test, with screenshots; the overlay × time-of-day matrix (3 × 5) renders non-blank frames on the recommended default look; the perf hard gate holds for every look until the pick, including the full overlay; a unit test checks that no view constructs a Three.js material directly. After the pick, only the picked look runs the full set (see [what is gated](#how-the-look-test-works-without-a-gate)).
- **Phone check:** which look makes you want to keep playing, and can you still read rivals, the cop and traffic at speed in each one?
- **Order:** at once.

### look-2 · The look switcher

- **Goal.** Flip style, overlay, proportions and palette live from the pause menu. [default]
- **Owns:** the switcher panel in `src/ui/`.
- **Builds:** the switcher, its recommended default, a live "slow" marker, and a "copy look" button that puts the current combination in the debug report, so the maintainer's pick can be pasted in chat like a tuning preset. The marker uses the p95 frame time of the current look measured on this device: `app/` owns the frame loop, samples it, and passes the number to `ui/` as plain data, because `ui/` may not import `dev/`. It shows "slow" when that p95 exceeds the frame budget. CI numbers are regression signals only and never phone evidence ([the architecture doc](../architecture.md#performance-budgets)), and the hard gate's draw-call and triangle counts cannot see the overlay's fill-rate cost, which is the real A16 risk. [default]
- **Automated acceptance:** a browser test flips every control and sees the rendered frame change (pixel difference above a threshold), and "copy look" gives an id that `packs:check` accepts.
- **Phone check:** switching is quick, and your pick is one tap to share.
- **Order:** as soon as look-1 has two looks.

### characters-1 · Eight rivals by design

- **Goal.** Each rival recognisable by shape, not only colour, in every look. [decided for character design as an identity source; default for the shape rule]
- **Owns:** the rider views. The riders' `look` blocks stay in files owned by the ai and cops lanes; this task proposes them by one-line PRs.
- **Builds:**
  - Procedural rider presets per rival: helmet shape, silhouette, jacket pattern, bike class and colours, from [the tone guide's looks](../tone-guide.md#the-rivals) (Deacon Vane's duster and chopper, Kevin's suit and scooter, and so on).
  - The proportions parameter (exaggerated or realistic) as rider data, so the look test is a data flip. [default, per [the rider format](../content-packs.md#rider-rivals-cops-player-presets)]
  - A character sheet for each rival in the lookbook: front, side and at-speed views, rendered from the game.
- **Automated acceptance:** a browser test renders all eight in a row in each look (all four until the pick), and a greyscale version of the same frame still shows eight distinct silhouettes (a simple outline-difference check between every pair).
- **Phone check:** at speed, can you tell Kevin from Dial-Up without looking at colour?
- **Order:** at once.

### identity-1 · Look and identity exploration, including AI concept art (side quest)

- **Goal.** Find the game's visual identity: concept art, a signature palette proposal, rider character sheets and zine-overlay references. [decided that identity comes from these four sources; default that this is a side-quest lane, per [the roadmap](../roadmap.md#side-quests)]

### camera-3 · Far-chase and helmet cameras

- **Goal.** The next two camera modes. [decided that all come, staged; default for M3]
- **Owns:** `src/camera/`.
- **Builds:** `farChase` and `helmet` modes from [the camera section](../architecture.md#camera), a camera setting, and a gamepad and keyboard binding to cycle modes. The helmet camera softens roll and FOV kick under M2's reduce-screen-shake setting; M5's fuller reduce-motion option takes over that job later. [default]
- **Automated acceptance:** each mode settles without overshoot and never reads anything but snapshot and road data.
- **Phone check:** is either one better than the low chase cam on the phone?
- **Order:** at once.
- **As built (head start, 2026-10-01)** [default]. Every number below is a `camera.*` tuning slider. Done, not phone-verified.
  - **Choosing the view.** `camera.mode` is the base view: 0 low chase (the default), 1 far chase, 2 helmet. It is the "View" slider in the tuning panel until ui adds a settings row. `FollowCamera` gains `view`, `setView` and `cycleView`. `bindViewKey(target, camera)` cycles on C (`KeyC`, unused by the product spec's key map) on whatever event target it is handed. It ignores repeats, Ctrl, Alt and Meta chords, and keys typed into a text field. The camera reads no browser global, so the key is live once `app/` binds it to the window. Look-back and the takedown framing sit on top of every view.
  - **Far chase** is the same rig at 11 m back and 4.2 m up (`camera.farDistanceM`, `camera.farHeightM`), aiming 28 m ahead (`camera.farLookAheadM`). The phone-shape extras (`camera.wideHeightM`, `camera.wideDistanceM`) apply to it too. Switching between it and the low chase cam blends on the critically damped springs, so it never overshoots.
  - **Helmet** puts the camera at the rider's eyes, 1.78 m up and 0.1 m forward in the rider model (`camera.helmetHeightM`, `camera.helmetForwardM`). It leans and turns exactly as render leans and turns the model, so render's 0.3 m near plane always hides the rider's own helmet. It looks along the road at a point 30 m ahead, 1.1 m up (`camera.helmetLookAheadM`, `camera.helmetAimHeightM`). It has a 70° field of view (`camera.helmetFovDeg`) and rolls with half the lean (`camera.helmetRollFraction`).
    - Under the reduce-shake setting, its roll and FOV kick shrink toward 30 % (`camera.helmetCalm`). At full reduce-shake they are 30 % of normal.
    - Switching into or out of the helmet view is a hard cut, because a blend would fly the camera through the rider. A takedown framing also cuts in from the helmet view.
    - Off the bike (tumbling, on foot or free), the helmet view shows the low chase framing, and it cuts back when the rider is riding again.
  - **Tests.**
    - `views.test.ts` (13 tests): the far-chase blend and the helmet roll and FOV settle without overshoot; the helmet sits within 5 cm of the rider's helmet centre, with all 192 corners checked against the near plane at 4 leans × 3 headings; reduce-shake leaves exactly the calm share; there are tests for the cuts, the off-bike fallback and the key.
    - `reaction-range.test.ts`: the 125 m oncoming-car check now runs for far chase (36 sightings, in view and clear of the rider) and for the helmet (36 sightings, in view, with every sight line passing above the bars).
    - The every-slider test now runs in all three views (38 declarations).
    - `boundaries.test.ts` still finds no browser or clock global in any camera file.
  - **Wiring left to other lanes.**
    - `app/`: call `bindViewKey(window, camera)` once.
    - input: a remappable `cycleCamera` key action and a gamepad button. The pause-screen key legend should then list C.
    - ui: a camera-view row in settings, saved with the other settings. It sets `camera.mode` or calls `setView`.
    - render (optional): hide the player's own rider parts in the helmet view, which would also free the eye height from the helmet box.

## Pipeline, data and QA tasks

### blender-1 · The Blender-script pipeline

- **Goal.** A headless Blender script builds a game asset, exports it, and the game loads it through the asset manifest. [default, from the blueprint roadmap] Extended Blender tooling beyond this is its own side quest; if that side quest delivers this first, this task is already done. [default, per [the roadmap](../roadmap.md#side-quests)]
- **Owns:** `tools/blender/`, `packs/base/assets/models/`.
- **Builds:**
  - An npm script (for example `asset:build`) that runs Blender headless from the `BLENDER_EXE` environment variable, with Python scripts in `tools/blender/` linted by `ruff` only ([repo layout](../engineering.md#repo-layout)).
  - One scripted asset, the starter bike, exported to GLB and validated. Its wheels stay separate nodes so they can spin. An earlier local probe found that a common mesh optimiser's default mode merged a bike's wheels into its body, so any optimising step keeps nodes. [default]
  - A manifest entry with a `baked` source and the code-made bike as its procedural fallback, switched in the bike's data (a one-line PR to the sim riders lane, which owns the bike files). [default, per [the asset manifest](../architecture.md#asset-manifest)]
  - CI does not run Blender. It validates the committed GLB and loads it; regenerating the GLB is a local step whose output is committed. Files over 1 MB go to the dataset repo instead. [default]
- **Automated acceptance:** the GLB passes validation; the pack check resolves its asset id; the game loads it and renders a non-blank frame; switching the bike's data back to procedural still works; the size check passes.
- **Phone check:** the scripted bike looks at least as good as the box one.
- **Order:** at once.

## M3 exit

### Automated exit criteria

M3 is **done** when these hold on `main`. [default]

1. The whole gate is green, with nonzero counts.
2. Every look renders a non-blank frame in the browser test, with screenshots, and the overlay × time-of-day matrix does so on the recommended default look. (From [the roadmap](../roadmap.md#m3--look).)
3. The perf hard gate holds for every look, including the one combined full-screen pass with the full overlay. (From the roadmap.) If the maintainer has picked a look by the exit, only the picked look is held to this and the rest are marked unmaintained.
4. The pipeline's scripted asset validates and loads through the asset manifest. (From the roadmap.)
5. The HUD presets validate, and the editor round-trips a layout. (From the roadmap.)
6. The eight rivals pass the greyscale silhouette check in every gated look.
7. Threats render out to at least 200 m in every gated look, with the scenery on.
8. The build is live on the game Space, with a player-facing `changes/` note.

### The M3 phone playtest

1. Which look makes you want to keep playing? Try each one with the overlay off, subtle and full.
2. In each look, can you still read rivals, the cop and traffic at speed?
3. Exaggerated or realistic riders?
4. Which palette, at which time of day?
5. Do the menus feel like a 90s zine?
6. Move the buttons where you want them.
7. Far chase or helmet: better than the low chase cam?
8. Do the Keys look like somewhere?
9. Open the lookbook, and say which concept art feels right.

Then paste the look from "copy look". It becomes the default look id in a data PR.

## Risks

- **Every look on the A16.** By the maintainer's report, the look lab's four styles rendered and ran fine on the phone, but no frame-rate number was recorded, and the real scene is heavier. [decided that the A16 is the benchmark] If a look misses the budget, quality tiers and dynamic resolution come early, as [the architecture doc](../architecture.md#quality-tiers-and-dynamic-resolution) allows.
- **The overlay costs a full-screen pass.** It is budgeted as part of the one combined pass; multi-pass effects stay high-tier only.
- **Identity doesn't appear on demand.** The maintainer found all four styles pleasing but without identity. The identity sources are decided; whether they produce identity is the playtest's answer. More identity rounds may follow in M4 through interludes and art, without holding M3.
- **AI concept art and public safety.** No real people, brands or insignia, labelled as AI-generated, and within the spend cap.
- **Blender on the machine.** The pipeline assumes a local Blender install reached through `BLENDER_EXE`. If agents can't reach it, the committed GLB still gates, and the task waits for a session where Blender is available.
