# Milestone 4: career and cast

> **In plain words.** Milestone 4 turns the one road into a whole game. You start a career, ride the first event while short prompts teach you the controls, earn cash, and buy better bikes. There are about ten events in three tiers: classic races, takedown hunts, cop escapes and one-on-one grudge matches, ending in a boss grudge race. All eight rivals are in, each with a style, a bike, trash talk that remembers what you did to them, and grudges that make them hunt you. Sgt. Pruitt and the other local cops chase, bust and fine you. Short comic-panel or fake-TV stills play between events. Now and then something strange happens mid-race: a hurricane gust, a parade, a boat rolling loose on its trailer, or a bounty on your head. The radio gets surf and rockabilly stations. Beating the boss plays a teaser for the next region, and you can keep playing. Progress saves on the phone, with a code you can copy as a backup. Agents also shortlist real names for the game, for you to pick before M5.

- **Owns:** `src/career/` (DOM-free), `packs/base/careers/`.
- **Key work:** tiers and advance rules; objectives across races; the boss; the ending teaser and free play afterwards [decided]; the race-first first run straight into the tutorial event [default]; the shop and the cash ledger (placing, takedowns, near-misses and style cash) [decided for the sources]; fines; the Road Trip failure mode as the default, with a fine capped at the cash you have [decided], and the `failureMode` field as a seam for the later Classic and Hardcore options [decided] and the proposed Chill ([failure states](../product-spec.md#failure-states)) [default]; grudge history, bark heard-counts and once-per-career flags as career state, of which the profile keeps the fields listed in [save-3](#save-3--the-profile-and-the-export-code), grudges included, so they survive closing the game [decided]; the resolved grudge table, as loaded from the save, handed into `SimConfig` at race start ([the rider format](../content-packs.md#rider-rivals-cops-player-presets)); unlocks (below).

- **Acceptance:** a headless career test has a bot play every event and the boss in the simulation, and the career ends in the teaser and free play; a Road Trip fine never takes cash below $0; a grudge built in one session is still there after a save and reload, and a rival who holds it targets the player more in the next race (printed); unit tests cover each advance rule and each `when.kind`.

The four locals come from the Keys pack, and the four regulars travel with the circuit. [decided for the 4 + 4 shape; default for who is which]
- **Acceptance:** in a seeded batch, a rival with a high grudge targets the player more than one with none (printed); a gang-up forms in a seeded scenario and a rival sides with the player in another; scripted races hash the same every run.
- **As built (head start, 2026-10-01; done, not phone-verified).** Built early, alongside M2 and M3, in the parallel head-start run. All [default] unless marked.
  - **Styles.** `src/sim/ai/styles.ts` registers every style id in the rider schema except `cop`: `heavy-hitter`, `weaver`, `showboat`, `grudge-keeper`, `scrapper`, `crowd-pleaser`, `crew-boss` and `racer`. Each is M1's two behaviour sets (brawler or racer) plus a few traits: the heavy hitter is slow off the line (throttle capped at 0.85 for the first 12 s); the weaver rides 3 % above pace and swerves across the road, not only inside its lane, with sudden jumps; the showboat never swings at the race leader and never at anyone healthier than him; the grudge-keeper counts every hit he takes; a player who has landed one is hunted for the rest of the race, and he swings 25 % more often at anyone per wrong they did him (up to 4) (his tally hunts players only, so it never pulls him off the player); the scrapper aims 12 % above pace for the first 12 s, never backs down and hunts whoever just hit her for 5 s; the crowd-pleaser stops fighting below 60 % health and rides away from the pack; the crew boss rides 2 % above pace, fights whoever is closing on her from behind first (a new `chaser` target preference) and never waits for the player. Every number is a constant in `styles.ts` or `index.ts`. The traits sit behind the `ai.styleQuirks` switch (the tuning panel's Rivals group), **off by default** until the maintainer tries it: with it off, the base race replays exactly as before (checked: three seeded bot races give the same finish order, hit count and motion digest as main), so playtest 1's difficulty and the seeded tests of the lanes running alongside hold. Turning it on by default is a one-line change. **Integration round (2026-10-01): now on by default** `[default]` (the maintainer wants fun and variety). In a seeded real race the weaver's swerve shows as the most sideways travel on a clear road (0.74 m/s against 0.56 m/s with the quirks off, over seeds 1-3) rather than a wider spread, and the heavy hitter covers about 287 m in the first 12 s against 395 m off (`tests/sim/app-cast-and-law.test.ts`). `buildSimConfig` now passes `rivals` and `preferredWeapon` too. A road weaver keeps its swerve inside its lane while another rider is within 10 m, so a bunched pack does not turn the swerve into random bumps.
  - **The cast as data.** The four regulars' files now carry their styles (Deacon heavy hitter, Dial-Up weaver, Chad showboat, Kevin grudge-keeper; their other numbers are unchanged. Playtest 1 said rivals stay as hard as they are: over riders-5's 16 seeds with the bot, rival hits on the player were Easy 94 and Hard 108 as riders-5 recorded them, and Easy 76 and Hard 114 with the quirks on (a local run, not CI, before the weaver's pack rule); Chad lands fewer, since he picks his fights). With the quirks on, Chad's `leader` preference is filtered out by his showboat rule. The three Keys locals are new rider files: Tammy Two-Stroke (scrapper, Swamp Kin), The Mayor (crowd-pleaser) and Mother Rust (crew boss, Swamp Kin's boss). Sgt. Pruitt stays the cops lane's file. The locals are in no event yet: events are the riders lane's.

### cops-3 · The law

- **Owns:** `src/sim/cops/`, the cop files and the law crews.
- **Key work:** the spawn mix (tier-rising, every race, chaos-summoned) with some randomness [decided]; the hidden chaos meter [default]; fines that grow with the tier [default]; the baton and taser, stealable [decided for the weapons; default for stealing from cops]; Sgt. Pruitt as the named county deputy [decided for Pruitt]; the Keys law as a mix of county deputies, state troopers and maybe a marine patrol, with parody names and fully parody forces [default]; made-up agency names.
- **Acceptance:** each spawn mode is exercised by a seeded test; chaos raises the spawn count (printed); the fine scales by tier; a stolen baton works like any weapon.
- **As built (head start, 2026-10-01; done, not phone-verified).** Built early in the parallel head-start run, in #157 (contract) and #170. All [default]; every number is a tuning slider.
  - **Not live in a race yet.** `buildSimConfig` (the app lane's) does not pass the event's `cops` block and tier, or the riders' `startingWeapon`, into `SimConfig` yet, so the race still fields one bare-handed Sgt. Pruitt. The exact mapping is in the head-start report's follow-ups. Everything below runs in the sim and its tests.
  - **The spawn mix.** `SimEventDef.cops` carries the event file's `cops` block (`mode`, `baseCount`, `tierScale`, `chaosSummon`, `randomness`) and `SimEventDef.tier` the tier. `every-race` brings `baseCount` of the fielded cops out of the lot, `tier-rising` brings `baseCount` + `tierScale` × (tier − 1), `chaos-summoned` brings `baseCount` (usually 0) and relies on chaos, and `none` brings nobody, chaos or not. Each still rolls the M2 spawn chance; they pull out `cops.waveGapS` (8 s) apart; `randomness` jitters the count and the gaps from the `cops` stream. Without a `cops` block the M2 rule stands: every fielded cop rolls to come out. Further cops park 8 m apart behind the lot. Siren events carry a `cause` (`every-race`, `tier-rising`, `chaos`).
  - **The hidden chaos meter.** With `chaosSummon`, or in `chaos-summoned`: 1 point per hit with a player in it (2 more when the target is a cop), 3 per player takedown, draining `cops.chaosDecayPerS` (0.1 a second). At `cops.chaosSummonAt` (10, jittered by `randomness`) the next cop in the lot is summoned: siren now, out after the siren lead.
  - **At most two chasing.** `cops.maxActive` (2, the plan's "at most 2 on screen"); the next cop waits in the lot, siren off, until a chase ends.
  - **Fines.** A bust's `fineCash` is the cop's `fineCash` × (1 + (tier − 1) × `cops.fineTierScale`), rounded; at the default 0.5 a $400 fine is $400 at tier 1, $600 at tier 2, $800 at tier 3. The bust event also carries `tier` and `fineBaseCash`, and the debug report's event line shows all three. There is no career cash yet, so this is an event seam: career-1 charges it, and Road Trip caps it at the cash you have.
  - **Armed cops.** A cop holding a weapon swings at the rider he chases when that rider is inside the weapon's reach, at most every `cops.swingEveryS` (6 s), so the M1 steal can take it off him mid-swing; a cop's landed hit on a player is softened by `combat.copOnPlayerScale` (0.5: damage, shove and stun). An unarmed cop never swings, as before. Measured with the bot over the 50 batch seeds, with the fields mapped as the follow-up will map them: the player is busted in 10 of 50 races with an unarmed Pruitt; an armed Pruitt swinging every 3 s at full strength was 20 of 50, over the 30 % limit; the shipped defaults give 9 of 50; two cops (baton and taser) with chaos summons give 13 of 50.

A draft second cop, Trooper Dalrymple of the parody Sunburn State Troopers, carries the taser; his id sorts after Pruitt's, so a one-cop event still fields Pruitt. No marine patrol: the sim has no boats.
  - **Tests.** `src/sim/cops/law.test.ts` (13 tests) exercises each mode (no block, every-race, tier-rising, chaos-summoned, none), the chaos meter (what counts, the drain, a lower threshold summons sooner), the jitter (same seed, same race; seeds differ), the cap, the fines and the armed cop's swing. It prints tier-rising tiers 1-5 as 1, 2, 2, 3, 3 cops, and chaos-summoned over 30 s as 0 cops with no chaos against 2 with a player hit every half second. The stolen baton is in weapons-2's tests below.
  - **Polish round (2026-10-01; done, not phone-verified).** The integration skeptic found that only the Keys cop was armed, the taser never shipped, and steal chances were rare (1 steal window in 5 seeded races). All [default]:
    - **Every region's cop is armed.** Deputy Lindqvist (Pacific Northwest) carries the taser and Officer Meter (San Francisco) the baton, beside Sgt. Pruitt's baton. The taser is live; Trooper Dalrymple stays a draft.

### weapons-2 · Every v1 weapon

- **Owns:** `src/sim/combat/`, `packs/base/weapons/`.
- **Key work:** club or pipe, chain, wasteland junk (for example Kevin's briefcase and The Mayor's campaign sign), the baton and the taser with a stun effect and charges [decided for the list; default for the examples]; roadside spawns; every weapon is data with a registered behaviour, so a new weapon that reuses one needs no code [decided].
- **Acceptance:** every weapon's steal window lints inside its wind-up; a scripted steal works for each weapon; a new weapon added as data only (a test fixture) plays without code changes.
- **As built (head start, 2026-10-01; done, not phone-verified).** Built with cops-3 above, in #157 and #170. All [default]; every feel number is a tuning slider.
  - **Registered behaviours.** `WEAPON_BEHAVIOURS` in `src/sim/combat/` is the closed list: `melee.swing` (M1's swing), `melee.wrap` (the chain: a landed hit also drags the target's speed by `combat.wrapDragMps`, 4 m/s) and `taser.stun` (a landed hit also stuns: no attacks and a wobble for the weapon's stun × `combat.stunScale`, and the target loses `combat.stunSpeedLoss`, 20 %, of its speed). An unknown or missing id is the swing. The `hit` event carries `dragMps` or `stunTicks`.
  - **Uses.** Charges and durability live on the weapon itself, so a stolen taser keeps what it has left. A charge goes as a held swing reaches its active moment; the swing that spends the last one finishes, then the weapon is gone. A durability hit goes per landed hit; the last one breaks the weapon on the blow. The `hit` or `attackMiss` of that swing carries `spent: true`, and a spent weapon never returns to the road.
  - **Starting weapons.** A rider's `startingWeapon` is in hand from the start, so the M1 steal takes a cop's baton or taser with no new code.
  - **Roadside spawns.** Each of the three spots draws its weapon from the `combat` stream, weighted by `spawn.roadsideWeight`; the cops' weapons weigh 0 and never lie on the road.
  - **The pack (drafts: dev and staging builds only).** Bike Chain (`melee.wrap`, long and slow, weight 2), Driftwood Club (the pipe's cousin, breaks after 10 hits, weight 2), Kevin's Briefcase (junk: light damage, a big shove, 5 hits, weight 1), Campaign Sign (junk: long reach, a wide shove, 3 hits, weight 1), Baton (cop-issue, weight 0) and Taser (`taser.stun`, 6 charges, a 0.9 s stun, long reach, weight 0). The lead pipe is M1's.

### modifiers-1 · Weird events (M4-late)

### radio-1 · Surf and rockabilly radio (M4-late)

- **Owns:** `src/audio/`, `packs/base/stations/`.
- **Needs:** assets-2 for the AI-generated tracks, which are long audio and must be `remote` assets. Code-made tracks are `procedural` manifest entries and need nothing from assets-2, so they can land first.
- **Key work:** stations as data ([the station format](../content-packs.md#radio-stations-reserved)); surf and rockabilly stations for the Keys first [decided]; the station switch on the pause menu (added by the ui lane, ui-4) and its binding (added by the input lane), each requested by a one-line PR; the original score stays available (in menus, and as its own choice). The tracks come from both routes: tracks agents write as code (like the engine synth) and AI-generated tracks, made on the local GPU or on Hugging Face first, and the maintainer cuts the ones they don't like with "cut this", the same way as rival lines [decided] (`milestones-radio-music-source`, cockpit answer, 2026-09-29). Both routes start small, a few tracks each, before any batch [default]. Each track is a vetoable item in its station file, with its own content reference ([the station format](../content-packs.md#radio-stations-reserved)), and the pause menu's station panel shows the playing track so a long-press can cut it [default]. Original music only, and every AI-generated track is labelled in `THIRD_PARTY_ASSETS.md`. Before generating anything, record the music model's licence in `THIRD_PARTY_ASSETS.md`; a non-commercial or unclear licence is not used *(local music models ship under varied licences; not researched here, unverified)*. [default] DJ lines come later with the AI barks. [decided]
- **Acceptance:** the station list for a region is derived from each station's `regions`; switching stations changes the playing track in a browser test; the music bus still follows its slider; a station has at least one code-made and one AI-generated track, each with its `origin`; a long-press on the playing track puts its content reference in the debug report, and a track marked `vetoed` never plays in a seeded session.
- **As built (head start, 2026-10-01, code-made route only)** [default]:
  - Two stations in `packs/base/stations/`: `keys-surf` ("Low Tide 91.7") and `keys-rockabilly` ("Grease Trap 1340 AM"), both regional to `florida-keys`, four code-made tracks each (`origin: "agent"`, `procedural: {preset, params}`). No AI-made track yet, so the "one AI-generated track per station" acceptance waits for assets-2.
  - `src/audio/radio-compose.ts` writes each song from its preset (`surf-trio`, `rockabilly-trio`), its params (`bpm`, `key`, `progression` or `form`, `drums`, `drop`, `band`) and a seed. The seed is the FNV-1a hash of the track's content reference, mixed with an optional `params.seed`, so a song never changes unless its reference or seed does. Melodies are random walks over chord tones, so they are original. Surf: 8 bars of straight sixteenths, a tremolo-picked lead, offbeat stabs or strums, a floor-tom roll back to the top and sometimes a tremolo slide down the string. Rockabilly: a 12- or 8-bar shuffle with a shuffled ride, a backbeat, a slapped walking or boogie upright bass, boogie double-stops or seventh chops, licks that bend the minor third and double in sixths, and the chromatic turnaround.
  - `src/audio/radio-synth.ts` plays them: guitars and basses are Karplus-Strong plucked strings rendered once per pitch, drums are synthesized like the score. Surf's spring reverb is three damped comb filters, because a 1.6 s convolver added about 1.5 s of render time per 20 s of music in an offline Chromium render on the dev machine, against about 0.2 s for the rest of the band. Rockabilly gets a 110 ms slapback.
  - `src/audio/radio.ts` derives a region's stations from each station's `regions` (genre stations with an empty list are heard everywhere), orders a session's playlist from a seed, loops each song `audio.radioLoops` times (3), skips `vetoed` tracks and tracks cut on this device, and builds the "cut this" flag (`{contentRef, raceId, tick}`, reference `base:station/<station>#<track>`).
  - The switch, until ui-4's station panel: the R key cycles score, each station, off; Shift+R skips a song; the `audio.radio` slider picks directly (0 off, 1 score, 2 and up the region's stations in id order). The music (score or radio) ducks under crash, takedown, rail and splash cues (`audio.duckLevel` 0.4, `audio.duckHoldS` 0.9 s), and `duck()` is there for barks. `audio.radioFx` scales the spring and slapback.
  - Still to wire, by their owners: app/ calls `setRegion`, `setStations` (the registry's) and `setRadioCut` (the settings record's veto refs); ui-4 shows `inspect().radio.nowPlaying` in the pause menu's station panel and stores `cutPlayingTrack(raceId, tick)`'s flag; the bark owner calls `duck()` when a bark shows; the content schema could declare `procedural` on station tracks (it passes today as a loose field).
  - Done, not phone-verified.

## M4 exit

### Automated exit criteria

M4 is **done** when these hold on `main`. [default; items 2–6 are from [the roadmap](../roadmap.md#m4--career-and-cast)]

1. The whole gate is green, with nonzero counts.
2. A headless career test has a bot play every event and the boss in the simulation, and the career ends in the teaser and free play.
3. Each objective type has unit tests.
4. Saves round-trip, export codes decode, and every golden save fixture migrates.
5. The pack check's bark coverage report shows that every rival has enough lines per trigger.
6. If modifiers shipped in M4: weird events replay deterministically.
7. The perf hard gate holds at the largest race field, and, if modifiers shipped in M4, with a modifier active.
8. The build is live on the game Space, with a player-facing `changes/` note.

Criteria 6 and the modifier part of 7 are conditional because the amendment put weird-event timing at "M4 or the shelf" [default], and a slipped extra must not hold M4 open. If they slip, they take these criteria with them, and the roadmap gets a matching small PR.

### The M4 phone playtest

From the roadmap, in plain words:

1. Start a career. Is the pacing right?
2. Do grudges show? Does a rival you wronged come for you, and say so?
3. Does a bust sting without ruining the evening?
4. Do rivals, signs, billboards and interludes make you laugh? Veto what misses.
5. Did a weird event surprise you in a good way?
6. Which radio station do you leave on?
7. Beat the boss. Does the teaser make you want Region 2?
8. Read the name shortlist, and pick one.

## Risks

- **M4 is the widest milestone.** It has the most content and three new lanes. The slip rules below protect the loop.
- **Grudges across sessions.** With short, bursty sessions, memory barks would rarely fire if grudges reset on close, which is why the career save keeps them [decided] (cockpit answer, 2026-09-29). The remaining risk is the opposite one: a grudge that never fades. The rider's `decayPerRace` handles that, and it is a tuning value.
- **Radio needs music, and files.** Stations are only as good as their tracks, and AI tracks are too big for git, so radio depends on assets-2 and on a music model whose licence allows a public game. If neither the code-made nor the AI route produces tracks good enough to keep, radio moves after v1 and the single score stays. [default]
- **Balance.** Bike prices, fines and pacing are guesses until played. The career's numbers are data, so tuning is a data PR.
- **Tone at volume.** More than a hundred bark lines, plus signs, billboards, interludes and weird events, all written by agents. The veto and the taste log exist for exactly this. [decided]

## If M4 runs long

- The loop never slips: the career, the four event types, the eight rivals, cops and busts, cash and three bikes, and saving with the export code. [default]
- The M4-late tasks may move to M5 or the idea shelf, by a small PR to this plan and the roadmap, in this order: [default]
  1. easter eggs beyond one of each kind;
  2. radio (and assets-2 with it);
  3. weird events beyond one of each kind;
  4. the joke rides;
  5. weird events altogether, and the first easter eggs, if the loop is still not merged.
