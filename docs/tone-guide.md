# throttlebrawl: tone guide

> **In plain words.** This guide says how throttlebrawl should sound and feel: what is funny here and what isn't. The humor is deadpan. The world is ours, only a little off. Rivals trash-talk, road signs are dry, billboards are satire, and crashes are absurd but never gory. Agents write rival lines, signs, billboards and interludes freely, as long as they stay inside this guide. The maintainer plays, laughs or doesn't, and vetoes what misses, in the game itself: long-press a bark subtitle, billboard or sign and choose "cut this". Every veto goes into the [taste log](#taste-log) at the bottom, so the next agent learns from it. Rival lines are tagged by situation and remember your history with that rival, so they never feel canned or repeat.

## The humor register

### In the maintainer's words [decided]

- On tone: "Probably obviously road rash homage but definitely room for flavor and humor. Maybe some fallout borderlands wild wasteland type stuff lmao who knows but nothing corny cheesy cringe etc if we can help it".
- The kinds of funny the maintainer picked: **deadpan road signs, rival trash talk, absurd physics, and satirical ads and billboards**.
- On memes: "could be kinda memeish", built on outdated, stale memes from early nerdy internet culture, "without being cringe" and "not patronizing". The maintainer added: "this will take taste ;)".
- On the eight drafted rivals: "Surprisingly these are all oddly promising lol" and "All still surprisingly flavorful and none too terrible lol".
- On calibration: the sample register was "About right". The maintainer said it stays open to change in every direction unless a more defined theme is chosen.
- On the originals: "don't feel too limited by ps1 and n64 … put our own spin on things in whatever ways are the most fun."

### The register, as the planners read it [default]

- **Deadpan first.** Play the joke straight. The sign doesn't know it's funny. The rival means every word.
- **Specific beats random.** A joke about a real kind of thing (timeshares, reply-all emails, dial-up modems, county bureaucracy) lands. "Lol random" doesn't.
- **Mean to characters, never to the player's intelligence.** Rivals insult your riding. The game itself never lectures, winks or explains a joke.
- **The world is a little off.** It's the Florida Keys, but a boat is parked in the fast lane and nobody mentions it. That's the "wild wasteland": rare, strange, unexplained.
- **Punch at types and institutions.** Aim at the streaming outfit, influencer culture, office life, small-town politics and tourist traps. Never aim at real people or groups.
- **Stale memes as nods, not punchlines.** The reference sets up the line, and the violence or the deadpan delivers it: "You've got mail. It's my boot." Keep them to about one line in ten. A quoted meme on its own isn't a joke.
- **Short.** Most lines fit in a bubble you can read at 100 mph (see [the bark system](#the-bark-system)).
- **Swearing.** Mild words are fine ("hell", "damn", "ass"). No f-words or slurs in v1. The taste log is where the maintainer tightens or loosens this.

## Do and don't

All examples are agent drafts [default].

| Surface | Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Road sign | `HURRICANE EVACUATION ROUTE. YOU ARE ALREADY LATE.` | `WATCH OUT!!! CRAZY BIKERS AHEAD LOL` | The sign plays it straight; the "don't" is shouting and winking. |
| Road sign | `GATOR CROSSING. GATOR HAS RIGHT OF WAY. GATOR KNOWS.` | `Drive safe! Gators are an endangered and important part of our ecosystem.` | Deadpan, not a lecture. |
| Road sign | `NEXT GAS 40 MI. NEXT HOPE 60 MI.` | `NEXT GAS 40 MI. HAHA GOOD LUCK` | Let the reader find the joke. |
| Billboard | `SUNSET SHORES TIMESHARES — OWN 1/52 OF PARADISE. FOREVER. LEGALLY.` | A real company's name or logo | Satire of a type, never a real brand. |
| Billboard | `INJURED ON A BRIDGE? YOU PROBABLY DESERVED IT. — THE LAW OFFICE OF NOBODY` | `Buy throttlebrawl merch!` | In-world, not a real ad. |
| Rival barb | `Per my last email: move.` | `I'm gonna get you, sucka!` | Specific to the character, not a generic threat. |
| Stale meme | `Buffering… buffering… hit.` (Dial-Up) | `All your base are belong to us.` quoted on its own | A nod that serves the character, not a recited meme. |
| Crash | The rider cartwheels over a boat trailer and lands in a bait cooler | Blood, broken bones, gore | Absurd physics; no gore. |
| Pedestrian | A fisherman on the old bridge dives into the water, still holding the rod | Hitting pedestrians for points | They dive clear cartoonishly; hitting them isn't the joke. |
| Interlude | A grainy still of Kevin's scooter with a caption: `KEVIN HAS SUBMITTED A FORMAL COMPLAINT.` | Three paragraphs of lore | Short stills plus text; lore lives in details. |
| Loss screen | `BUSTED. FINE: $400. SGT. PRUITT DID NOT LOOK UP.` | `Oh no! Better luck next time, champ!` | Never patronizing. |

## The bark system

A bark is a short rival line shown as a text bubble in milestone 1 and voiced later. [decided] The maintainer asked for barks that are **contextual and non-repetitive**, built from **tagged lines plus memory** (grudge-history references), with an AI tie-in later. [decided]

### Writing rules [default]

1. **One line, one beat.** Aim for 42 characters or fewer; keep every line to 80 or fewer (the pack check warns above 80). A bubble stays up for the longer of 2 seconds and the line's length divided by 15 characters per second, so a 42-character line shows for about 2.8 seconds. (Streaming subtitle guides cap adult programs at 20 characters per second for viewers who aren't steering a bike *(unverified; recalled, not fetched)*.) A new bark never starts while a bubble is still up, and a short quiet gap follows every bark.
2. **Sentence case.** At most one exclamation mark. No emoji.
3. **In character.** If the line would work for any rival, rewrite it until it wouldn't.
4. **Every line has a trigger, and conditions where it needs them.** Use the triggers and condition facts listed in [the content-pack doc](./content-packs.md#line-fields). A line with no trigger doesn't load.
5. **Enough lines per trigger.** By milestone 4, each rival needs at least 5 lines for common triggers (hit landed, hit taken, overtaking) and at least 2 for rare ones, so repetition doesn't show. Milestone 1 needs one line per trigger.
6. **Memory lines need plain siblings.** Memory lines use conditions like "this rival holds a grudge of 4 or more" or "you took this rival down before". Each one needs a plain sibling line for the same trigger, so a fresh career still gets a bark.
7. **Subtitled always.** Voiced lines keep their text, for the subtitles setting.
8. **Pass the hard lines.** Check every line against [Hard lines](#hard-lines) before committing it.

### Where the mechanics live

The data format, the trigger list, the condition facts, the cooldowns and the line-picking algorithm are all defined in [the content-pack doc](./content-packs.md#bark-sets-and-line-selection). This guide sets only the writing intent those mechanics serve [default]:

- **Contextual.** A line that fits the moment beats a generic one. The picker favors specific and memory-aware lines.
- **Non-repetitive.** A line isn't heard again soon, lines heard often fade, and silence beats a repeat.
- **Memory.** Rivals remember your history with them for the whole career, across play sessions, because grudges are saved with the career [decided] (cockpit answer, 2026-09-29): hits, takedowns, steals, and who beat whom last time. Rivals also remember each other, which gives them lines about each other.
- **Not drowning the race.** At most one bubble at a time. The race is louder than the chatter.

### Memory slots, a proposed extension [default]

The sample grudge lines in [The rivals](#the-rivals) use slots such as `{place}` (a landmark name, like "the Seven Mile Bridge") and `{count}` (how many incidents). The v1 content-pack format picks lines by conditions, but it doesn't fill slots. Until it adds slot filling:

- write each memory line without slots, and name no place or count unless a `when` condition pins it (a line about "the bridge" would state a false memory after an incident at the motel);
- keep the slotted versions in this guide as drafts for when the format supports whitelisted slots, each with a plain fallback line.

### AI tie-in, later [decided]

- Barks are generated offline first. Agents or local models write lines into content packs, and the maintainer vetoes. Live, race-reactive generation is optional and later.
- Offline or live, generated lines follow the same tags, rules and hard lines. The taste log is part of their prompt. [default]

## Hard lines

These never ship, whatever the joke. [default]

- No gore, and no hitting pedestrians or animals for points. They dive clear. [decided]
- No real people as targets or characters: no politicians, celebrities or real influencers. A guest "celebrity" rider is always a fictional celebrity, never a real person.
- No real brands, logos or company names in satire. Parody a type, not a trademark.
- No "Road Rash" in names or branding. [decided] "Inspired by Road Rash" is fine in prose, such as the readme, the credits and these docs. [decided]
- No reproducing Road Rash's characters, lines, track names or HUD.
- No slurs, and no jokes whose target is a real group's identity.
- Nothing corny, cheesy, cringe or patronizing. [decided]

## Taste log

The maintainer's calls on content go here, so agents learn the taste instead of re-guessing it. [decided that there is a taste log; default for the format]

### How to use it [default]

- Whenever the maintainer vetoes, tweaks or praises a piece of content, the agent handling that feedback adds an entry here in the same change that applies it.
- **In-game veto.** The maintainer can veto in the game: long-press a bark subtitle, billboard or sign and choose "cut this" (and, from M4, a radio track: long-press it in the pause menu's station panel [decided]). The flag arrives in the debug report; the agent removes the item from the game (its `status` is set to `vetoed`, and it stays in the file) and adds an entry here. Every bark, sign and billboard carries a content ID so the flag names it exactly. Mid-race both thumbs are busy, so the long-press is guaranteed on the pause screen's "recently seen" list, in replays and in interludes. [decided for the veto; default for where it is guaranteed]
- Before writing content, read the whole log. Its lessons outrank the examples in this guide.
- **Never re-propose vetoed content**, and never propose a close variant of it.
- When three or more entries point the same way, promote the lesson into the guide above and note that in the entry.
- Quote the maintainer's words exactly. Don't paraphrase them into something stronger.
- Record a vetoed line in two places. The line itself gets `status: "vetoed"` (a whole entry, such as a rival or a bark set, gets `meta.status: "vetoed"`), the machine-readable record the generator reads as a negative example (see [the content-pack doc](./content-packs.md#ai-generated-batches)). This log records the *lesson*, which is what a person or an agent needs to write the next line.
